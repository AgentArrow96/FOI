EXAM PREPARATION

Dangerous Confusions

Most wrong answers swap two ideas that sound almost identical but have different economic consequences. Learn the boundary between each pair.

Risk tolerance vs risk capacity

TolerancePsychological willingness to accept swings in value.
CapacityFinancial ability to absorb losses: horizon, income, buffers. A young investor can have high capacity but low tolerance.
{{see:l1_lifecycle}}

Liquid vs safe

LiquidCan be converted to cash quickly with little loss of value; a volatile stock can trade millions of shares a day.
SafeMultidimensional: default risk, price volatility, purchasing power. Cash: low default risk, but inflation can erode it.

Direct vs indirect investment

DirectYou own a claim on a security or property yourself.
IndirectYou own an interest in a professionally managed collection (a fund), not its underlying stocks.
{{see:l1_classify}}

Primary vs secondary market

PrimaryNew issues: the issuer receives the capital (IPO, rights offering, private placement).
SecondaryResales between investors: the seller receives the money. Still vital for liquidity and pricing.
{{see:l2_market_grid}}

Money vs capital market

MoneyShort-term securities (maturity within about a year), e.g. a new 90-day T-bill.
CapitalLong-term securities: stocks and bonds. A government security is not automatically long-term.

Broker vs dealer

BrokerAgent: brings buyer and seller together; no inventory.
DealerPrincipal: buys at the bid, sells at the ask, carries inventory risk.
{{see:l2_broker_dealer}}

Third vs fourth market

ThirdInstitutions trade listed stocks through market makers that are not exchange members.
FourthInstitutions trade directly with each other (ECNs), bypassing market makers.

Margin vs amount borrowed

Margin(V − D) / V: the investor's equity as a share of current value. It falls when the price falls.
Debit balanceD, the loan: it stays fixed, which is why equity absorbs the whole loss.
{{see:l2_margin_sheet}}

Margin purchase vs short sale

MarginOwe money; profit when prices rise; loss can exceed initial equity.
ShortOwe shares; profit when prices fall; gain capped, loss unlimited.
{{see:l2_short_steps}}

Descriptive vs analytical information

DescriptiveFactual data about past behaviour: "revenue grew 15%".
AnalyticalCurrent data plus projections and recommendations: "growth will slow to 5%".

Stop vs stop-limit order

Stop (stop-loss)Becomes a market order when triggered: likely to exit, but the price can gap below the stop.
Stop-limitBecomes a limit order: price protected, but may not fill if prices keep falling.
{{see:l3_order_matrix}}

Simple vs compound interest

SimpleInterest on the original principal only: RM1,000 at 10% for 3 years → RM1,300.
CompoundInterest on interest too: → RM1,331.
{{see:l4_tvm_timeline}}

Annuity vs mixed stream

AnnuityEqual amounts at equal intervals: use the annuity formula.
Mixed streamUnequal amounts: discount each cash flow separately.
{{see:l4_annuity_vs_mixed}}

Expected vs required return

ExpectedWhat you forecast the investment will earn.
RequiredThe minimum that compensates for its risk: Rf + risk premium. Accept when expected ≥ required.
{{see:l4_required_stack}}

HPR vs IRR

HPRTotal return over the holding period; ignores the time value of money.
IRR (yield)Compound annual rate; compares different holding periods; assumes reinvestment at the same rate.

Business vs financial risk

BusinessCan the operations earn enough? Falling profits, lost market share.
FinancialCan the firm meet its financing obligations? More debt = more financial risk.

Standard deviation vs coefficient of variation

σAbsolute dispersion of returns around the mean.
CV = σ / meanRelative dispersion: risk per unit of return. Use it when averages differ.

Systematic vs unsystematic risk

UnsystematicFirm-specific (strikes, lawsuits); diversifiable.
SystematicMarket-wide (war, inflation, politics); not diversifiable; the only risk CAPM rewards.
{{see:l5_risk_floor}}

Beta vs standard deviation

BetaSensitivity to market movements (systematic risk).
σTotal volatility. A low-beta stock can still be very volatile.

SML vs efficient frontier

SMLRequired return vs beta; CAPM pricing benchmark.
Efficient frontierExpected return vs σp; best portfolio combinations (MPT).
{{see:l5_frontier}}

Number of stocks vs diversification

Counting40 bank stocks are still one big bet on banking.
CorrelationDiversification comes from holdings whose returns don't move together.
{{see:l5_offset}}
