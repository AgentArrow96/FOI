QUESTION LOGIC

How to Attack the Test

Investing questions reward identifying the economic problem first (timing, risk compensation, who owes what, which risk) and punish grabbing the biggest number or a familiar keyword.

Eight-question scenario method

  1. Who is the investor: goal, horizon, liquidity need, capacity for loss?
  2. What exactly is owned or owed: debt, equity, derivative; money or shares borrowed?
  3. Which market: new issue or resale; short- or long-term?
  4. Who is on the other side, broker or dealer, and at bid or ask?
  5. Do cash flows happen at different dates? Move them to one date first.
  6. One cash flow, equal payments or a mixed stream?
  7. What return is required for this risk, and does the expected return clear it?
  8. Which risk matters: total (σ, CV) or systematic (β)? For one asset or a portfolio?

Watch the trap words

  • "Always", "guaranteed", "necessarily", "only" usually make a reasonable statement wrong.
  • "Higher return" alone never proves a better investment: at what risk, for whom?
  • "Stop-loss guarantees": a stop is a trigger, not a price.
  • "Protected by SIPC" covers broker failure, not market losses.
  • "40% return": over how many years? Annualise before comparing.
  • "Diversified with 40 stocks": check what they have in common.
  • "Low beta, so safe": beta ignores firm-specific volatility.

Calculation questions

  • Write the formula, substitute, then interpret the number in one sentence.
  • Margin: equity changes, the loan does not; return is on initial equity, after interest.
  • Short sale: profit = (sale − buy-back) × shares; margin = (funds held − buy-back cost) / buy-back cost.
  • TVM: match r and n to the compounding period (r/m and mt).
  • σ in the chapter uses n − 1; CV = σ / mean.
  • CAPM: Rf + β × (E(Rm) − Rf): beta multiplies the premium.
  • Portfolio return and beta are weighted averages; portfolio σ needs the correlation.

Subjective answers: define, explain, apply, evaluate

  1. Define the concept in the chapter's terms.
  2. Explain the mechanism: why the relationship exists.
  3. Apply it to the investor or numbers in the scenario.
  4. Contrast with the nearest competing concept or alternative.
  5. Evaluate: limitations, assumptions, and a justified conclusion.

"Margin increases return" earns little. "Margin magnifies the percentage change in the investor's equity in both directions, because the loan stays fixed while gains and losses fall on a smaller equity base; interest and margin calls reduce its attraction" earns the marks.

Final-hour revision

  1. Read the master cheat sheet, including every formula.
  2. Review the dangerous confusions.
  3. Take one 30-question mixed test.
  4. Retry mistakes only, reading each exam trap.
  5. Outline one mock subjective answer per chapter and compare it with the model answer.
