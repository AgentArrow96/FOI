Chapter 1: The Investment Environment

Investment = an asset expected to produce positive income and/or an increase in value. Defined by purpose, not object. Return = income + change in value.
Classify on several dimensions at once: securities/property · direct/indirect · debt/equity/derivative · low/high risk · short/long term · domestic/foreign.
Debt = creditor (lend for interest + repayment). Equity = owner. Derivative = value derived from an underlying asset (options, futures).
Risk = uncertainty surrounding the return, not just "losing money".
Individuals are net suppliers of funds; businesses and governments net demanders. Funds flow directly, through institutions or through markets.
Institutional investors manage other people's money (banks, insurers, mutual funds, pension funds). Mutual fund vs ETF: same pooling idea; ETFs trade on exchanges all day.
Seven steps: prerequisites → goals → plan → evaluate return & risk → select → diversify → manage (compare actual vs expected, correct).
What matters is what you keep: after-tax return, type of income, timing of gains. Life cycle: growth (youth) → consolidation (middle age) → income (retirement).
Interest rates ↑ ⇒ bond prices ↓. Liquidity = fast conversion with little loss of value. Short-term investments: liquid, low default risk, low return, inflation risk.

Chapter 2: Securities Markets and Transactions

Money market = short-term; capital market = long-term. Primary = new issue (issuer gets the cash); secondary = resale (seller gets the cash). Two separate questions.
Secondary markets give liquidity and continuous pricing. Primary offerings: public, rights offering, private placement.
IPO: underwriting (bank buys the issue, bears resale risk) · prospectus · red herring (preliminary) · quiet period · road show. Syndicate shares risk; selling group distributes; paid via the price discount.
Broker = agent (matches buyer and seller). Dealer = principal (market maker). Bid = dealer buys; ask = dealer sells; bid < ask; spread = ask − bid.
Third market: institutions via non-member market makers. Fourth market: institutions directly with each other (ECNs).
International investing: diversification, but currency and government-policy risk. $(1+R_{home}) = (1+R_{asset})(1+R_{FX})$.
Laws by purpose: 1933 disclosure · 1934 SEC · 1938 self-regulation · 1940 funds and advisers · 1975 no fixed commissions · 1988 insider trading · 2002 Sarbanes–Oxley.
Margin $= \frac{V-D}{V}$ = equity / value. Margin magnifies gains and losses on equity; costs interest; margin calls. Call value $V^* = \frac{D}{1-m}$.
Short sale: borrow → sell → buy back → return. Profit = (sale − buy-back) × shares. Gain capped, loss theoretically unlimited.

Chapter 3: Information and Transactions

Online investing: easier research and trading, but no live "safety net", sceptical of free advice, beware frequent trading and margin.
Descriptive = past facts. Analytical = current data + projections + recommendations. Facts ≠ forecasts ≠ opinions.
Regulation FD: material information to professionals and public at the same time. Watch pump-and-dumps; ask the five defensive questions.
Indexes: gauge conditions, benchmark performance, study cycles. DJIA = 30 stocks; S&P 500 = broader, more representative. Construction (price/value/equal weight) changes the result.
Bond yield = total return of buying at the current price and holding to maturity. Coupon ≠ yield.
Brokers: full-service (advice, high fees), premium discount, basic discount. Street name = broker registered, client beneficial owner. Churning = excessive trading for commissions.
Accounts: cash · margin · custodial (minor) · wrap (manager, flat fee). Round lot = 100 shares; odd lot = fewer.
Market order = execution first. Limit = price first, may not fill. Stop → market order when triggered (no price guarantee). Stop-limit → limit order (may not fill). Day, GTC, fill-or-kill.
SIPC = broker failure, not market losses or bad advice. Mediation = informal, non-binding; arbitration = formal, binding if agreed. Registration ≠ competence.

Appendix 4A: Time Value of Money

Money today is worth more than the same amount later. Simple interest on principal only; compound interest also on accumulated interest.
$FV = PV(1+r)^n$ · $PV = \frac{FV}{(1+r)^n}$. Compounding moves money forward; discounting moves it back.
$r\uparrow, n\uparrow \Rightarrow FV\uparrow$ and $PV\downarrow$. Never compare cash flows at different dates directly.
Annuity = equal payments at equal intervals; ordinary annuity pays at the end of each period. $FV = PMT\frac{(1+r)^n-1}{r}$ · $PV = PMT\frac{1-(1+r)^{-n}}{r}$.
Mixed stream: $PV = \sum \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}$ (no shortcut). Example: 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 at 8% → 193.51.
$m$ compounding periods a year: $FV = PV(1+\frac{r}{m})^{mt}$; effective rate $(1+\frac{r}{m})^m - 1$ rises with frequency (5% → 5.063%, 5.094%, 5.120%, 5.125%, 5.127%).
Calculator: N, I, PV, PMT, FV, CPT; cash paid out is negative.

Chapter 4: Return and Risk

Total return = income + capital gain/loss. Historical ≠ expected ≠ required. Expected = forecast; required = hurdle.
Required return = real rate + inflation premium + risk premium = $R_f + RP$; $R_f$ = real rate + inflation premium. Real return ≈ nominal − inflation.
Satisfactory investment: PV of benefits ≥ PV of costs.
$HPR = \frac{\text{income} + (P_1 - P_0)}{P_0}$; easy, but ignores time. Yield / IRR: $r = (FV/PV)^{1/n} - 1$; accept if IRR ≥ required return; assumes reinvestment at the same rate.
Growth rate $g = (D_n/D_0)^{1/n} - 1$ (0.92 → 1.85 in 9 years ≈ 8.07%).
Nine risks: business · financial · purchasing power · interest rate · liquidity · tax · event · market · currency exchange.
Standard deviation = absolute dispersion (sample, n − 1). $CV = \sigma / \bar r$ = risk per unit of return. They can rank investments differently.
Risk-averse: more return for more risk (the usual assumption). Risk-indifferent: flat. Risk-seeking: accepts less return for more risk.

Chapter 5: Modern Portfolio Concepts

Efficient portfolio = highest return for a given risk. Growth-oriented vs income-oriented portfolios.
$E(R_p) = \sum w_iE(R_i)$ (weighted average). $\sigma_p = \sqrt{w_A^2\sigma_A^2 + w_B^2\sigma_B^2 + 2w_Aw_B\sigma_A\sigma_B\rho_{AB}}$ (not an average).
Correlation −1 to +1: the lower ρ, the bigger the risk reduction. ρ = −1 can eliminate risk only with the right weights ($w_A\sigma_A = w_B\sigma_B$).
Total risk = diversifiable (unsystematic, firm-specific) + nondiversifiable (systematic, market-wide). Diversification removes only the first.
Beta = sensitivity to the market; market β = 1. $\beta_p = \sum w_i\beta_i$. β ≠ σ.
CAPM: $R_i = R_f + \beta_i[E(R_m) - R_f]$. Only systematic risk is rewarded. Alpha = expected − required.
SML: required return vs beta; intercept $R_f$, slope = market risk premium. Above = undervalued; below = overvalued.
Efficient frontier: return vs σp; the leftmost edge of the feasible set; the investor's utility curve picks the point.
Traditional (variety, industries, familiar names) vs MPT (expected returns, σ, correlations). The chapter recommends combining both.
