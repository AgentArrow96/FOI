# Chapter 4 — Difficult mode

## D1 · Remember
Which statement defines the time value of money?

A. Money loses value only when prices rise.
B. As long as money can earn interest, its value depends on the point in time at which it is received.
C. Interest is paid only on the original deposit.
D. Cash flows in the future are always larger than cash flows today.

Answer: B
Why: A dollar (or ringgit) received sooner can start earning interest sooner, so it is worth more than the same amount later. A describes inflation, C simple interest, and D is not true in general.

## D2 · Remember
Which equation gives the **required return** in the chapter's model?

A. Required return = real rate × inflation premium × risk premium
B. Required return = real rate + expected inflation premium + risk premium
C. Required return = expected return − risk premium
D. Required return = risk-free rate − risk premium

Answer: B
Why: The three components add. Because the risk-free rate equals real rate plus inflation premium, this simplifies to R<sub>f</sub> + risk premium.

## D3 · Remember
What does the **coefficient of variation** measure?

A. Absolute dispersion of returns
B. The sensitivity of a stock to the market
C. The slope of the security market line
D. Relative dispersion: standard deviation per unit of average return

Answer: D
Why: CV = σ / mean return. Standard deviation is the absolute measure (A); beta and the SML belong to Chapter 5.

## D4 · Understand
Why does the present value of a future payment fall when the discount rate rises?

A. Because the payment itself shrinks.
B. Because inflation rises with the discount rate.
C. Because a higher opportunity rate means less money today is needed to grow into the same future amount.
D. Because compounding becomes simple interest.

Answer: C
Why: PV = FV / (1 + r)<sup>n</sup>. A larger r makes the denominator larger. Economically, if you can earn more elsewhere, you need to put less aside now.

## D5 · Understand
Why can holding-period return (HPR) mislead when the holding period exceeds a year?

A. HPR does not account for the time value of money, so returns over different horizons are not comparable.
B. HPR ignores capital gains.
C. HPR only works for bonds.
D. HPR cannot be negative.

Answer: A
Why: A 40% return over one year and a 40% return over five years have the same HPR but very different annual performance. Compound annual yield (IRR) fixes this.

## D6 · Understand
Which pair correctly contrasts business risk and financial risk?

A. Business risk comes from interest rates; financial risk comes from inflation.
B. Business risk affects bonds only; financial risk affects stocks only.
C. Business risk concerns uncertainty in the firm's earnings and operations; financial risk concerns uncertainty from its debt-and-equity financing mix.
D. They are the same risk described differently.

Answer: C
Why: Two identical restaurants share similar business risk, but the one with heavy debt carries more financial risk.

## D7 · Apply
RM5,000 is deposited for 4 years at 6% compounded annually. What is the future value?

A. RM6,200.00
B. RM6,312.38
C. RM6,000.00
D. RM6,524.77

Answer: B
Why: FV = 5,000 × (1.06)<sup>4</sup> = 5,000 × 1.262477 = **RM6,312.38**. A uses simple interest-like growth; C is simple interest at 5%; D uses 7%.

## D8 · Apply
What is the present value of RM10,000 due in 3 years at an 8% discount rate?

A. RM7,938.32
B. RM7,500.00
C. RM8,000.00
D. RM8,264.46

Answer: A
Why: PV = 10,000 / 1.08<sup>3</sup> = 10,000 / 1.259712 = **RM7,938.32**. D discounts for only 2 years.

## D9 · Apply
A share is bought at RM80, pays a RM4 dividend, and is worth RM84 at year-end. What is the HPR?

A. 5%
B. 10%
C. 15%
D. 4%

Answer: B
Why: HPR = [income + (P<sub>1</sub> − P<sub>0</sub>)] / P<sub>0</sub> = (4 + 4) / 80 = **10%**. A counts only the dividend or only the gain.

## D10 · Analyze
RM1,000 is deposited at the end of each year for 8 years at 8% (future value RM10,636.63). Which statement is correct?

A. Interest earned is RM2,636.63, because earlier deposits compound for longer.
B. Interest earned is RM8,000.
C. The future value equals RM8,000 × 1.08.
D. The last deposit earns seven years of interest.

Answer: A
Why: Total deposits = RM8,000, so interest = 10,636.63 − 8,000 = **RM2,636.63**. The first deposit compounds seven years and the last (made at the valuation date) earns nothing.

## D11 · Analyze
Investment A has a mean return of 10% and σ of 20%. Investment B has a mean of 20% and σ of 30%. Which is correct?

A. A is riskier on both measures.
B. A has lower σ, but B has lower relative dispersion (CV 1.5 versus 2.0).
C. B has lower σ and lower CV.
D. CV cannot be computed for these data.

Answer: B
Why: CV(A) = 20 / 10 = 2.0; CV(B) = 30 / 20 = 1.5. Absolute and relative risk can rank investments differently.

## D12 · Analyze
An asset is bought for RM1,000 and sold for RM1,400 five years later, with no income. Which statement is correct?

A. Its compound annual return is 8%.
B. Its compound annual return is 40%.
C. Its HPR is 40%, and its compound annual return is about 6.96%.
D. Its HPR is 6.96%.

Answer: C
Why: HPR = 400 / 1,000 = 40%. Annual yield = 1.4<sup>1/5</sup> − 1 ≈ 6.96%. Dividing 40% by 5 (= 8%) ignores compounding.

## D13 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "An investment expected to return 12% is attractive, because 12% is a good return."

A. Incomplete: it depends on the required return for its risk; if the required return is 14%, 12% is inadequate.
B. Correct.
C. Correct if the investment is a stock.
D. Incorrect, because only returns above 20% are attractive.

Answer: A
Why: Expected return is a forecast; required return is the hurdle. No return is good or bad in isolation.

## D14 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "Investment B has the lower standard deviation, so it is the better investment."

A. Incomplete: low risk is not automatically superior if the return is far lower; the decision depends on required return, risk preference and objectives.
B. Correct.
C. Incorrect, because σ is irrelevant.
D. Correct if B is a bond.

Answer: A
Why: The chapter teaches optimising the relationship between return and risk, not minimising risk.

## D15 · Evaluate
Bank A offers 12.5% compounded annually. Bank B offers 12% compounded monthly. Which is better?

A. A, since 12.5% > 12%.
B. B, since monthly compounding always beats annual compounding.
C. They are equal.
D. B: its effective annual rate is (1.01)<sup>12</sup> − 1 ≈ 12.68%, above A's 12.5%.

Answer: D
Why: Compare effective annual rates, not stated rates. Option D overgeneralises: monthly compounding beats annual compounding only at the same stated rate; here the stated rates differ, so the calculation decides.

## D16 · Create
You need RM50,000 in 5 years and can earn 8% a year. Which equal year-end contribution is required (approximately)?

A. RM10,000
B. RM6,250
C. RM7,500
D. RM8,523

Answer: D
Why: FV annuity factor = (1.08<sup>5</sup> − 1) / 0.08 = 5.8666. PMT = 50,000 / 5.8666 ≈ **RM8,523**. A ignores interest.

## D17 · Create
Which sequence best builds a decision framework for choosing among three investments, per the chapter?

A. Choose the highest expected return.
B. Choose the lowest standard deviation.
C. Estimate expected returns; determine the required return for each risk; measure risk with SD and CV; compare expected with required return; choose the one consistent with the investor's risk preference.

Why: This is the chapter's four-step decision process, combining return, risk and investor preference.

## D18 · Create
An investor estimates a real return of 2.5%, expected inflation of 3% and a risk premium of 4% for a particular security. What required return should the investor use as the discount rate or hurdle?

A. 5.5%
B. 7.5%
C. 4.0%
D. 9.5%

Answer: D
Why: R<sub>f</sub> = 2.5% + 3% = 5.5%; required return = 5.5% + 4% = **9.5%**. A is the risk-free rate only.

## X1 · Extension · Industry · Apply
A company borrows RM100,000 at 8% and repays it in 5 equal year-end instalments. Approximately what is each instalment?

A. RM25,046
B. RM20,000
C. RM28,000
D. RM21,600

Answer: A
Why: The loan is the present value of an ordinary annuity: PMT = 100,000 / [(1 − 1.08<sup>−5</sup>) / 0.08] = 100,000 / 3.99271 ≈ **RM25,046**.

## X2 · Extension · Industry · Understand
A retiree holds a bond paying a fixed RM5,000 a year while inflation rises sharply. Which risk is most directly at work?

A. Business risk
B. Purchasing-power risk
C. Event risk
D. Financial risk

Answer: B
Why: The nominal payment is unchanged, but its real value falls. Fixed-income securities and deposits are the chapter's examples of purchasing-power risk.

## X3 · Extension · CFA · Apply
A stated rate of 8% is compounded quarterly. What is the effective annual rate?

A. 8.00%
B. 8.50%
C. 8.24%
D. 8.32%

Answer: C
Why: (1 + 0.08/4)<sup>4</sup> − 1 = 1.02<sup>4</sup> − 1 = 1.082432 − 1 ≈ **8.24%**. More frequent compounding raises the true rate.

## X4 · Extension · CFA · Analyze
At 9% compounded annually, roughly how long does money take to double?

A. About 4 years
B. About 16 years
C. About 12 years
D. About 8 years

Answer: D
Why: Solve 1.09<sup>n</sup> = 2: n = ln 2 / ln 1.09 ≈ 8.04 years. (The "rule of 72" gives 72 / 9 = 8.)

## X5 · Extension · Case competition · Analyze
An investment costs RM12,000 and pays RM4,000, RM5,000 and RM6,000 at the ends of years 1–3. At a 10% required return its NPV is about +RM276. What happens at a 12% required return?

A. NPV rises, because the payments are unchanged.
B. NPV stays exactly the same.
C. NPV turns negative (PV of benefits ≈ RM11,828 < RM12,000), reversing the decision.
D. The investment becomes risk-free.

Answer: C
Why: At 12%: 4,000/1.12 + 5,000/1.12<sup>2</sup> + 6,000/1.12<sup>3</sup> ≈ 3,571 + 3,986 + 4,271 = 11,828. A thin positive NPV has little room for adverse changes in assumptions.

## X6 · Extension · Case competition · Evaluate
Fund A gained 50% over 4 years. Fund B earned 11% a year for 4 years. Which is better on a compound annual basis?

B. A, because 50% is larger than 11%.
C. They are equal.
D. A, because it earned its return faster.

Answer: A
Why: Always annualise before comparing horizons.

## X7 · Extension · Master's · Analyze
Why does using IRR to evaluate a bond implicitly assume reinvestment of coupons at the IRR itself?

## X8 · Extension · Master's · Evaluate

## X9 · Extension · PhD · Analyze
An investment returns +50% in year 1 and −50% in year 2. The arithmetic average return is 0%. What happens to wealth?

A. It is unchanged.
B. It rises by 25%.
C. It falls to 75% of the starting amount, a compound annual return of about −13.4%.
D. It falls to 50% of the starting amount.

Answer: C
Why: 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75. The compound (geometric) annual return is √0.75 − 1 ≈ −13.4%. This is why averages of annual returns overstate compound growth when returns are volatile.

## X10 · Extension · PhD · Evaluate
When is the coefficient of variation a poor measure?

A. When returns are always positive.
B. When the mean return is near zero or negative, so the ratio becomes huge, undefined or hard to interpret.
C. When there are more than 10 observations.
D. When returns are in ringgit.

Answer: B
Why: CV = σ / mean. A mean near zero makes CV explode; a negative mean makes its sign meaningless. Use σ and judgement in such cases.

## X11 · Extension · Postdoc · Evaluate
Textbooks use the 3-month Treasury bill as the risk-free rate. When discounting a 25-year project, what is the main conceptual concern?

A. T-bills pay no interest.
B. No concern: the risk-free rate never changes.
C. T-bills are riskier than stocks.
D. A maturity mismatch: a short-term rate reflects short-term conditions, so the discount rate for long cash flows should reflect longer-horizon rates and the project's own risk.

Answer: D
Why: The risk-free rate is a proxy. Required return also needs a risk premium appropriate to the project and the horizon.

## X12 · Extension · Postdoc · Create
Which exam question best separates a student who memorised formulas from one who understands TVM?

A. "State the PV formula."
B. "Define an annuity."
C. "What does PMT stand for?"
