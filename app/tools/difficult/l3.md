# Chapter 3 — Difficult mode

## D1 · Remember
Which kind of information reports **factual data on the past behaviour** of the economy, a market, an industry or a company?

A. Analytical information
B. Speculative information
C. Descriptive information
D. Insider information

Answer: C
Why: Descriptive information is factual data on past behaviour. Analytical information combines current data with projections and recommendations about potential investments.

## D2 · Remember
What does Regulation FD require?

A. That companies pay dividends every year.
B. That brokers register with the SEC.
C. That critical company information be disclosed to investment professionals and the public at the same time.
D. That all trades be reported to an exchange within a day.

Answer: C
Why: Regulation FD (fair disclosure) prevents selective disclosure of material information to a privileged group. Broker registration (B) is a different requirement.

## D3 · Remember
Which description of a **limit order** matches the chapter?

A. An order to buy at or below a specified price, or to sell at or above a specified price; if the limit is not met, the order is not filled.
B. An order to buy or sell at the best price available when it is placed.
C. An order that becomes a market order once a trigger price is reached.
D. An order that expires at the end of the day.

Answer: A
Why: B is a market order, C is a stop (stop-loss) order and D describes a day order, which is a time condition rather than a price condition.

## D4 · Understand
Why does the chapter warn that online investing removes a "safety net"?

A. Because there is no live broker to question or slow down a decision, so mistakes and impulsive trades are easier to make.
B. Because online brokers are always unregulated.
C. Because online trades are slower.
D. Because online investing prohibits limit orders.

Answer: A
Why: The convenience of acting instantly also removes useful friction: a conversation with a broker. The chapter pairs this with warnings about "free" advice, frequent trading and margin.

## D5 · Understand
Why is the S&P 500 considered more representative of the overall U.S. market than the DJIA?

A. It is calculated using a different calculator.
B. It includes 500 companies from major industries, versus the DJIA's 30 stocks.
C. It contains only technology stocks.
D. It is published more often.

Answer: B
Why: Breadth is the point: more companies across more industries. The DJIA's 30 high-quality stocks make it popular but narrower.

## D6 · Understand
What is **street name** registration?

A. Registering securities in the investor's home address.
B. Registering securities in the brokerage firm's name while they are held in trust for the client, who remains the beneficial owner.
C. A way to hide ownership from regulators.
D. Ownership transferred permanently to the broker.

Answer: B
Why: The broker is the registered holder for convenience, but the investor keeps the economic ownership. Registration convenience is not economic ownership.

## D7 · Apply
A stock trades near RM30. You want to buy but refuse to pay more than RM29.50. Which order fits?

A. Market order
B. Sell stop order at RM29.50
C. Limit buy order at RM29.50
D. Fill-or-kill market order

Answer: C
Why: A limit order protects the price: it executes only at RM29.50 or lower. A market order prioritises execution and could fill above RM29.50. A sell stop is for selling.

## D8 · Apply
An investor buys 1,000 shares at RM5.00 and sells at RM5.30. Commissions are RM30 on each trade. What is the net profit?

A. RM300
B. RM240
C. RM270
D. RM210

Answer: B
Why: Gross profit = 1,000 × 0.30 = RM300. Total commissions = 2 × 30 = RM60. Net = 300 − 60 = **RM240**.

## D9 · Apply
You own a stock bought at RM20, now RM25, and place a sell stop at RM22. Overnight bad news makes the stock open at RM18. What most likely happens?

A. The order sells at exactly RM22.
B. The order becomes a buy order.
C. The order is cancelled and no sale occurs.
D. The stop triggers and becomes a market order, so the sale executes near RM18, below the stop price.

Answer: D
Why: The stop price is a trigger, not a guaranteed price. Once triggered the order becomes a market order and fills at the best available price, which here is far lower.

## D10 · Analyze
Why can a stop-**limit** order fail to protect an investor on a sharp fall?

A. Because it becomes a market order.
B. Because it becomes a limit order once triggered, and if the price falls through the limit without trading at that level, the order may not fill at all.
C. Because it can only be used for buying.
D. Because it executes before the stop price.

Answer: B
Why: A stop-limit gives price control but gives up execution certainty. A plain stop gives execution at an uncertain price.

## D11 · Analyze
A broker earns a commission on every trade and recommends that a retiree "rotate" their portfolio monthly. What risk is most relevant?

A. Churning: the broker's incentive (more trades, more commissions) may not align with the client's interest
B. Currency risk
C. Event risk
D. Reinvestment risk

Answer: A
Why: Excessive trading to generate commissions is churning. Transaction costs are a hurdle every trade must clear, so frequent trading usually reduces net returns.

## D12 · Analyze
A balanced fund of Malaysian small-cap stocks returned 12% while the S&P 500 returned 18%. Which analysis is best?

A. The fund underperformed by 6 percentage points.
B. The comparison is unreliable: the S&P 500 does not represent the fund's investment universe, so a small-cap benchmark should be used.
C. The fund beat the market, because small caps are riskier.
D. Benchmarks are irrelevant.

Answer: B
Why: Benchmark selection must match the exposure being evaluated. Comparing a Malaysian small-cap portfolio with the S&P 500 is not automatically meaningful.

## D13 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "A stop-loss order guarantees that my maximum loss is the difference between my purchase price and the stop price."

A. Correct.
B. Incorrect: the stop becomes a market order when triggered, so a gap can fill well below the stop; a stop-limit may not fill at all.
C. Correct, but only for day orders.
D. Incorrect, because stops do not exist for long positions.

Answer: B
Why: Stop price = trigger, not guaranteed sale price. This is among the chapter's most testable ideas.

## D14 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "SIPC protection means my investments cannot lose money if my broker fails."

A. Incorrect: SIPC protects against certain losses from a broker's financial failure, but not against market losses, bad advice or churning.
B. Correct.
C. Correct, because SIPC insures market value without limit.
D. Incorrect, because SIPC only protects the broker.

Answer: A
Why: Investor protection means protection against certain institutional failures, not against ordinary investment losses. The slides cite historical limits (USD 500,000 for securities, USD 250,000 for cash).

## D15 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "Advisors are registered with the SEC, so their advice is reliable."

A. Incorrect: registration means meeting regulatory requirements; the slides note that no law or regulator guarantees competence, so experience and credentials still matter.
B. Correct.
C. Correct, because registration tests investment skill.
D. Incorrect, because advisors cannot register.

Answer: A
Why: Licensed does not imply good. A good advisor also asks many questions about the client's goals and circumstances before recommending anything.

## D16 · Create
Design a sound process for a student wanting to invest in a technology company after seeing an online recommendation.

A. Buy immediately before the price moves.
B. Copy a friend's portfolio.
C. Ask an anonymous forum for a price target and buy the next day.
D. Read the company's filings, review its financial performance, separate facts from forecasts, compare with competitors and a relevant benchmark, set an entry price, place a limit order and monitor the position.

Answer: D
Why: It uses primary sources, separates descriptive from analytical information, uses a benchmark and chooses an order type that matches the price condition.

## D17 · Create
An investor sees a stock at RM28 but is only willing to buy at RM25 or below, and is happy to wait several weeks. Which order set-up fits best?

A. Market order, day
B. Fill-or-kill market order
C. Sell stop at RM25
D. Limit buy at RM25, good-'til-canceled

Answer: D
Why: The price condition is "RM25 or lower" (limit) and the time condition is "willing to wait" (GTC). A market order would buy at RM28.

## D18 · Create
Which design would best protect an investor who has shorted a stock at RM50 against a sharp rise?

A. Buy stop at RM60
B. Sell stop at RM40
C. Limit sell at RM60
D. No order

Answer: A
Why: A buy stop above the current price is activated if the price rises to RM60, triggering a purchase that closes the short. The slides note stops can be used to buy stock to limit risk on short positions.

## X1 · Extension · Industry · Understand
Why might an equal-weighted index and a market-value-weighted index of the same stocks give different returns in the same year?

A. They use different stocks.
B. Value-weighted indexes ignore market values.
C. Equal-weighted indexes ignore prices.
D. In a value-weighted index the largest companies dominate; in an equal-weighted index every stock counts the same, so a rise in small stocks affects the two differently.

Answer: D
Why: Weighting method determines which stocks drive the index. An index is not neutral: its construction methodology affects what it measures.

## X2 · Extension · Industry · Apply
A price-weighted average has three stocks priced RM10, RM20 and RM90. Which stock dominates, and what happens if the RM90 stock rises 10% while the others are unchanged?

A. The RM10 stock dominates; the average rises about 15%.
B. All stocks are equal; the average is unchanged.
C. The RM90 stock dominates; the average rises from 40 to 43, about 7.5%.
D. The RM20 stock dominates; the average rises 1%.

Answer: C
Why: A price-weighted average is the average of prices: (10 + 20 + 90) / 3 = 40. After the rise: (10 + 20 + 99) / 3 = 43, a 7.5% increase. The highest-priced stock has the biggest influence.

## X3 · Extension · CFA · Apply
A bond with a face value of 1,000 and a 6% coupon trades at 950. Which statement is correct?

A. The current yield equals 6% because the coupon is fixed.
B. The yield is below 6%.
C. The yield to maturity is above 6%, because the buyer receives the coupon plus a price gain to par at maturity.
D. Yield cannot be compared with the coupon rate.

Answer: C
Why: The chapter notes bond yield is total return from buying at the current price and holding to maturity: coupon plus the gain from 950 to 1,000. Coupon ≠ yield.

## X4 · Extension · CFA · Analyze
An investor places a market order to sell 10,000 shares of a thinly traded stock. Which outcome is the main risk?

A. Poor execution: the order is large relative to market depth, so successive fills may occur at progressively worse prices.
B. The order will never execute.
C. The order becomes a limit order.
D. The order expires at day end.

Answer: A
Why: A market order prioritises execution, not price. In a thin market, a large order moves the price against the seller; a limit order would control price at the cost of fill certainty.

## X5 · Extension · Case competition · Analyze
A forum user posts a strong buy case and mentions owning the stock. Which is the best first step?

A. Treat the post as opinion from an interested party, verify its claims against filings and primary evidence, and ask what the poster gains if readers buy.
B. Buy because the post is detailed.
C. Ignore all online information.
D. Sell the stock short immediately.

Answer: A
Why: Source incentives matter (pump-and-dump). The skill is to separate evidence from narrative, not to dismiss information.

## X6 · Extension · Case competition · Evaluate
A brokerage offers zero commissions but earns money by routing orders to market makers. Evaluate "free trading means no trading costs".

A. Correct.
B. Incomplete: an explicit commission is only one cost; spreads and the execution price also matter, and zero commission may encourage more frequent trading.
C. Incorrect, because market makers do not exist.

Why: Transaction costs include commissions, bid–ask spreads and poor execution. Lower visible friction can raise trading frequency, the behavioural issue the chapter flags.
