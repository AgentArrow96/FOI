# Chapter 1 — Difficult mode

## D1 · Remember
Which definition of an investment matches the chapter exactly?

A. Any asset into which funds can be placed with the expectation that it will generate positive income and/or increase its value.
B. Any asset bought with borrowed money in the hope of a capital gain.
C. Any security traded on an organised exchange.
D. Any asset whose price has risen over the past year.

Answer: A
Why: The chapter defines an investment by the **expectation of positive income and/or an increase in value**. B adds borrowing and drops income; C restricts investments to exchange-traded securities, but property and tangibles can be investments too; D confuses past performance with the defining expectation.

## D2 · Remember
In the chapter's classification of investors, which statement is correct?

A. Individuals are typically net demanders of funds; businesses are net suppliers.
B. Governments are typically net suppliers of funds.
C. All three groups are net suppliers when interest rates are high.
D. Individuals are typically net suppliers of funds; businesses and governments are typically net demanders.

Answer: D
Why: Individuals, as a group, save more than they borrow, while businesses and governments typically need outside funds for production and public projects. "Net" describes the group's typical aggregate role: individuals still borrow for houses and cars.

## D3 · Remember
Which pair lists the chapter's **two disadvantages** of short-term investments?

A. Low liquidity and high default risk
B. Price volatility and currency risk
C. High taxes and high transaction costs
D. Low levels of return and loss of potential purchasing power from inflation

Answer: D
Why: The slides list advantages (high liquidity, low risk of default) and disadvantages (low return, possible loss of purchasing power from inflation). A reverses the advantages; C and B are not the chapter's listed drawbacks.

## D4 · Understand
Why do short-term, highly liquid, low-default-risk investments usually offer low returns?

A. Because investors value safety and liquidity, so issuers need to offer less compensation.
B. Because the government caps their interest rates.
C. Because they are taxed more heavily than stocks.
D. Because their prices are more volatile than those of stocks.

Answer: A
Why: Desirable features (safety, liquidity) reduce the compensation investors demand. This is the general logic of the risk-return trade-off: you are paid for accepting undesirable characteristics. B and C are not the chapter's reasoning; D is false, as short-term instruments are less volatile.

## D5 · Understand
An equity mutual fund and an ETF holding the same stocks differ mainly in that:

A. the ETF is a direct investment, while the mutual fund is indirect.
B. the ETF's shares trade on an exchange while the market is open, unlike conventional mutual fund shares.
C. the mutual fund cannot hold more than 30 stocks.
D. only the ETF provides diversification.

Answer: B
Why: Both pool investors' money into a professionally managed portfolio (both are indirect and both diversify). The ETF changes the **trading structure**: its shares trade on exchanges during market hours, according to the slides.

## D6 · Understand
Why does the chapter place "meeting investment prerequisites" before selecting investments?

A. Because an investor without emergency funds or protection may be forced to sell investments at a bad time, whatever their quality.
B. Because regulators forbid investing without insurance.
C. Because prerequisites raise expected returns.
D. Because selecting investments is the least important step.

Answer: A
Why: The prerequisites (funds for emergencies, protection against death, illness and disability) prevent a mismatch between the investor's circumstances and the asset's characteristics. Without them, a cash need can force a sale during a market fall, the "survive first" logic.

## D7 · Apply
A 26-year-old has stable income, a six-month emergency fund, no debt and is saving for retirement in 35 years. Which allocation best fits the chapter's life-cycle framework?

A. Mostly short-term deposits to preserve capital.
B. Only options and futures, because young investors should maximise risk.
C. Only bonds, because bond prices never fall.
D. Mostly growth-oriented investments such as common stocks, because the long horizon allows time to recover from falls.

Answer: D
Why: Young investors with the prerequisites met have high risk capacity and a long horizon, so growth assets are relatively suitable. A sacrifices growth needlessly; C is false (bond prices fall when rates rise); B confuses "can take more risk" with "should take maximum risk".

## D8 · Apply
An investor earns an 8% nominal return, pays 25% tax on it, and inflation is 4%. What is the **approximate** real after-tax return?

A. 4.0%
B. 6.0%
C. 2.0%
D. −2.0%

Answer: C
Why: After-tax return = 8% × (1 − 0.25) = 6%. Real ≈ 6% − 4% = **2%**. A ignores tax; B ignores inflation; D subtracts the tax rate and inflation in the wrong way.

## D9 · Apply
Classify a directly held, newly issued 5-year Malaysian government bond for a Malaysian investor.

A. Security, indirect, equity, short-term, foreign
B. Property, direct, debt, long-term, domestic
C. Security, direct, derivative, short-term, domestic
D. Security, direct, debt, long-term, domestic

Answer: D
Why: A bond is a **security** (not property), held **directly**, it is **debt** (you lend to the issuer), its maturity exceeds one year (**long-term**), and for a Malaysian investor it is **domestic**.

## D10 · Analyze
Two investors buy the same equity fund. Investor A needs the money for a house deposit in 9 months; Investor B is saving for retirement in 30 years. Which statement best analyses the situation?

A. The fund is equally suitable for both because they hold the same asset.
B. Suitability differs: the same asset suits B's long horizon but exposes A to a possible large fall just before the money is needed.
C. The fund is more suitable for A because A needs higher returns sooner.
D. Neither should invest, because equity funds are risky.

Answer: B
Why: Suitability is a function of objective, horizon, risk capacity and liquidity needs, not of the asset alone. A cannot afford a fall before the purchase; B has time to recover. D replaces analysis with a blanket rule.

## D11 · Analyze
An ETF can be sold within seconds on any trading day. Why can it still be **unsuitable** for money needed next month?

A. ETFs are illiquid.
B. ETFs cannot be sold until they mature.
C. Market liquidity is not price stability: the ETF can be sold quickly, but possibly at a much lower value than was invested.
D. ETFs pay no dividends.

Answer: C
Why: The chapter defines liquidity as quick conversion **with little or no loss in value**. An equity ETF meets the speed condition but not the price-stability one over short horizons. That distinction is what makes it unsuitable for near-term needs.

## D12 · Analyze
Market interest rates rise from 5% to 7%. What happens to the price of an existing bond paying a fixed 5% coupon, and why?

A. It rises, because higher rates make all bonds more valuable.
B. It falls only if the issuer's credit rating changes.
C. It stays at par, because coupon payments do not change.
D. It falls, because its fixed 5% coupon must compete with new bonds paying 7%, so buyers pay less for it.

Answer: D
Why: The bond's cash flows are fixed; when new bonds offer more, the old bond's price must fall until its return is competitive. Interest rates and bond prices move in **opposite** directions.

## D13 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "Cash is the safest investment, so a retirement fund for someone aged 30 should be held entirely in cash."

A. Correct: cash has no risk of any kind.
B. Incorrect, because cash has high default risk.
C. Partly right on default risk, volatility and liquidity, but wrong overall: over 30+ years inflation can steadily erode purchasing power, so cash alone is unlikely to be suitable.
D. Correct, because retirement funds must never fluctuate.

Answer: C
Why: Safety is multidimensional. Cash is very safe in nominal terms but carries purchasing-power risk, which matters most over long horizons. A good answer credits what is true and rejects the "safest in every sense" claim.

## D14 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "Investment X (expected 15%) is better than Investment Y (expected 7%)."

A. True, since 15% > 7%.
B. True only if X's taxes are lower.
C. It cannot be judged without comparing the risks taken and the investor's objectives, horizon and liquidity needs.
D. False, because low-return investments are always safer.

Answer: C
Why: Return cannot be evaluated independently of risk and suitability. D swaps one blanket rule for another: a low return does not guarantee low risk.

## D15 · Evaluate
A broker recommends a high-growth technology fund to a client in their first meeting, without asking about the client's goals or circumstances. What is the strongest criticism?

## D16 · Create
A 35-year-old wants a house deposit in two years and to retire in 30 years. Which plan best applies the chapter?

A. Separate the goals: liquid, low-volatility short-term investments for the deposit, and a long-term, growth-oriented portfolio for retirement.
B. Keep everything in cash until the house is bought, then start investing.
C. One aggressive portfolio for both goals, since retirement is the longer goal.
D. Put both goals into whichever asset had the highest return last year.

Answer: A
Why: One investor can have several goals with different horizons, liquidity needs and capacities for loss, so each needs a matching strategy. C risks the deposit; B wastes the long retirement horizon; D chases past returns.

## D17 · Create
Design the order of an investment plan for a new graduate with RM15,000 savings, unstable freelance income and no insurance, who wants to "start investing in stocks".

A retiree depends partly on portfolio withdrawals. Which combination of objectives should a portfolio for them emphasise, per the chapter?

## X1 · Extension · Industry · Understand
A wealth manager describes a client as having "high risk tolerance but low risk capacity". What does this mean in practice?

A. The client is psychologically comfortable with volatility but financially unable to absorb large losses, so the portfolio should be constrained by capacity.
B. The client can afford large losses but dislikes them.
C. The client should take maximum risk.
D. The two terms mean the same thing.

Answer: A
Why: Tolerance is psychological willingness; capacity is financial ability (horizon, income stability, buffers, obligations). Professionals generally let the **lower** of the two constrain the portfolio. The chapter's life-cycle logic is mainly about capacity.

## X2 · Extension · Industry · Apply
A Malaysian bank offers a savings account at 2.5% when inflation is 3%. A client says it is "risk-free". How should an adviser respond?

A. Agree completely.
B. It is close to free of default and volatility risk, but the expected real return is about −0.5%, so purchasing power is expected to fall.
C. Say the account has high default risk.
D. Say it is riskier than equities in every respect.

Answer: B
Why: Real ≈ 2.5% − 3% = −0.5%. Separating nominal safety from real economic safety is the professional answer.

## X4 · Extension · CFA · Analyze
Two bonds are identical except that Bond A matures in 2 years and Bond B in 20 years. Market rates rise by 2 percentage points. Which is most likely?

A. Both fall by the same percentage.
B. Neither changes, because coupons are fixed.
C. Bond A's price falls by more, because it matures sooner.
D. Bond B's price falls by more, because its distant cash flows are discounted over more periods.

Answer: D
Why: The chapter states rates and bond prices move oppositely. Extending its logic with TVM (Appendix 4A): the further away the cash flows, the more a rate change affects their present value, so long bonds are more rate-sensitive.

## X5 · Extension · Case competition · Analyze
A start-up founder holds 90% of their wealth in the start-up's shares and asks whether to buy more "because I believe in the company". What is the strongest diagnosis?

A. Buy more; conviction reduces risk.
B. The founder's wealth is already concentrated: their income and wealth depend on the same company, so diversifying away from it reduces unnecessary concentration risk.
C. Sell everything immediately.
D. Concentration only matters for institutional investors.

Answer: B
Why: Diversification is about avoiding unnecessary concentration. A founder's job and wealth are exposed to the same firm-specific risk; belief does not remove that risk. C is an overreaction; a reasoned rebalancing is the answer.

## X7 · Extension · Master's · Analyze
Why is age only a **proxy** in life-cycle investing rather than a decision rule?

A. Because the real drivers are horizon, human capital (future earnings), obligations and liquidity needs, which correlate with age but differ between individuals.
B. Because older investors always take more risk.

Evaluate the claim: "Because institutional investors manage more money, they are always better investors than individuals."
