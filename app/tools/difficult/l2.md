# Chapter 2 — Difficult mode

## D1 · Remember
Which statement defines the **secondary market** correctly?

A. The market where short-term securities are traded.
B. The market in which securities are traded after they have been issued.
C. The market where new issues are sold to the public.
D. The market for foreign securities.

Answer: B
Why: Secondary = trading after issuance. A is the money market; C is the primary market; D is not a separate category in the chapter.

## D2 · Remember
What is a **red herring**?

A. The final prospectus filed after the offering.
B. A preliminary prospectus made available during the waiting period.
C. A public advertisement announcing a completed issue.
D. An illegal promotion of a stock.

Answer: B
Why: The red herring is the preliminary prospectus. The public announcement of an issue and its underwriters is the **tombstone** (C).

## D3 · Remember
Which law established the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)?

A. Securities Exchange Act of 1934
B. Securities Act of 1933
C. Maloney Act of 1938
D. Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002

Answer: A
Why: 1933 required full disclosure for new issues; 1934 created the SEC; 1938 allowed industry self-regulation (NASD); 2002 tightened accounting and audit rules after corporate fraud.

## D4 · Understand
Why are secondary markets economically important even though issuers receive no money from resales?

A. Because they set the coupon rate on new bonds.
B. Because they eliminate investment risk.
C. Because issuers receive a commission on each resale.
D. Because they provide liquidity and continuous pricing, which make buying new issues more attractive.

Answer: D
Why: The slides list liquidity and continuous pricing as the secondary market's role. An exit route and a market price make investors more willing to buy in the primary market.

## D5 · Understand
In an underwritten offering, what does the investment bank mainly take on?

A. The risk that the issuer's profits fall in future years.
B. The legal liability for the issuer's debts.
C. The risk of reselling the issue to the public at a price above what it paid.
D. The risk of currency movements.

Answer: C
Why: Underwriting = the bank buys the issue at an agreed price and bears the risk of reselling it. It is compensated through the discount (spread) on the offering price.

## D6 · Understand
Why does a margin investor's **percentage** gain or loss exceed the stock's percentage move?

A. Because the broker adds a bonus to margin accounts.
B. Because the stock price is quoted on margin.
C. Because margin accounts pay higher dividends.
D. Because the gain or loss is measured on the investor's equity, which is smaller than the position, while the loan stays fixed.

Answer: D
Why: The same ringgit gain or loss is divided by a smaller equity base. The loan does not change with the price, so equity absorbs the full move.

## D7 · Apply
A dealer quotes a share at RM4.85 bid / RM4.90 ask. You sell 2,000 shares. How much do you receive, ignoring commissions?

A. RM9,800
B. RM9,700
C. RM9,750
D. RM9,900

Answer: B
Why: You sell to the dealer at the **bid**: 2,000 × 4.85 = RM9,700. Buying would cost the ask: 2,000 × 4.90 = RM9,800 (A).

## D8 · Apply
You buy 200 shares at RM40 using 60% initial margin. The price later rises to RM46. Ignoring interest and dividends, what is your return on invested equity?

A. 15.0%
B. 9.0%
C. 25.0%
D. 37.5%

Answer: C
Why: Position = RM8,000; equity = 0.60 × 8,000 = RM4,800. Gain = 200 × 6 = RM1,200. Return on equity = 1,200 / 4,800 = **25%**. The unlevered return is 15% (A).

## D9 · Apply
You short 300 shares at RM25 and cover at RM31. Ignoring costs, what is the result?

A. Profit of RM1,800
B. Profit of RM9,300
C. Loss of RM7,500
D. Loss of RM1,800

Answer: D
Why: Short profit = (sale price − buy-back price) × shares = (25 − 31) × 300 = **−RM1,800**. A rising price hurts the short seller.

## D10 · Analyze
A margin account holds shares worth RM12,000 with a loan of RM6,000. The price falls 25%. What happens to the margin?

A. It stays at 50% because the loan is unchanged.
B. It falls to 25%.
C. It rises to 66.7%.
D. It falls to 33.3%: equity drops to RM3,000 on a value of RM9,000.

Answer: D
Why: New V = 9,000, D = 6,000, equity = 3,000. Margin = 3,000 / 9,000 = **33.3%**. The stock fell 25%, but equity fell 50% (from 6,000 to 3,000).

## D11 · Analyze
You buy 100 shares at RM60 with a RM3,000 loan. The maintenance margin is 25%. Below roughly what share price is a margin call triggered?

A. RM30.00
B. RM40.00
C. RM45.00
D. RM22.50

Answer: B
Why: $V^* = D / (1 - m) = 3{,}000 / 0.75 = 4{,}000$; per share 4,000 / 100 = **RM40**. Check: at RM40, equity = 1,000 and margin = 1,000 / 4,000 = 25%.

## D12 · Analyze
Two pension funds trade a large block of shares directly with each other through an electronic network, with no market maker. Which market is this?

A. Primary market
B. Fourth market
C. Third market
D. Over-the-counter dealer market

Answer: B
Why: The fourth market is institutions trading directly with each other, often via ECNs, bypassing market makers. In the third market, a non-member market maker is involved.

## D13 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "Short selling and buying on margin carry the same risk because both involve borrowing."

A. Correct.
B. Incorrect, because shorting involves no borrowing.
C. Incorrect: margin borrows money and profits when prices rise, its loss limited by the price reaching zero; shorting borrows shares and profits when prices fall, with a theoretically unlimited loss as prices rise.
D. Correct, because both require a broker.

Answer: C
Why: Both create obligations, but different ones (owe money vs owe shares) with different payoff shapes. The unlimited-loss asymmetry of the short sale is the key point.

## D14 · Evaluate
A U.S. stock gains 12% in dollars while the dollar falls 10% against the ringgit. Evaluate the Malaysian investor's claim: "I made 12%."

A. Wrong: the ringgit return is (1.12)(0.90) − 1 ≈ 0.8%.
B. Correct; currency doesn't matter.
C. Wrong: the ringgit return is 22%.
D. Wrong: the ringgit return is −10%.

Answer: A
Why: Home-currency return compounds the asset return and the currency change: 1.12 × 0.90 = 1.008, about **+0.8%**. Foreign asset return ≠ domestic investor return.

## D15 · Evaluate
A friend says: "I'm certain this stock will rise, so I'll use the maximum margin available." What is the strongest evaluation?

A. Leverage raises the sensitivity of equity to forecast errors, adds interest cost and margin-call risk: a correct long-term view can still end in a forced sale after a short-term fall.
B. Sensible, because confidence reduces risk.
C. Sensible, because margin calls only happen in bear markets.
D. Wrong only because margin interest is tax-deductible.

Answer: A
Why: Certainty about direction does not remove uncertainty about the path. Margin turns a temporary fall into a possible permanent loss if the investor is forced out.

## D16 · Create
A company needs RM800 million quickly and fears weak demand for its first share issue. Which arrangement best addresses the risk?

A. Sell the shares itself through its website.
B. Issue a red herring and skip the road show.
C. Use an investment bank to underwrite the issue, with a syndicate to share the risk and a selling group to widen distribution.
D. List directly on the fourth market.

Answer: C
Why: Underwriting transfers the resale risk to the bank; a syndicate spreads a large exposure; a selling group reaches more investors. The road show (dropped in B) helps gauge demand and set the price.

## D17 · Create
An investor expects a stock to fall but wants to cap the loss if it instead rises sharply. Which combination fits the chapter's tools?

A. Buy the stock on margin with a sell stop below the price.
B. Short the stock and place a buy stop above the current price.
C. Short the stock with no protective order.
D. Buy the stock with cash and a limit order.

Answer: B
Why: The short position expresses the bearish view; a buy stop above the price triggers a purchase to close the short if the price rises, limiting the otherwise unlimited loss (a Chapter 3 order type).

## D18 · Create
You are designing a lesson to show why the **secondary** market matters to a firm that never sells shares there. Which example makes the point most directly?

## X1 · Extension · Industry · Understand
Why do dealers widen their bid–ask spreads in volatile, thinly traded stocks?

A. Their inventory risk and the difficulty of finding the other side are greater, so they need more compensation for providing liquidity.
B. Regulators require it.
C. Volatile stocks pay higher dividends.
D. Spreads are fixed by the exchange.

Answer: A
Why: The spread compensates dealers for liquidity provision and inventory risk. More risk and less trading means a wider spread, a higher transaction cost for investors.

## X2 · Extension · Industry · Apply
A broker's maintenance margin is 30%. A client's long margin position is worth RM50,000 with a RM40,000 loan. How much must the client deposit (as cash that reduces the loan) to restore 30%?

A. RM15,000
B. RM5,000
C. RM10,000
D. Nothing

Answer: B
Why: Current margin = 10,000 / 50,000 = 20%. Needed: (50,000 − D) / 50,000 = 0.30, so D = 35,000. Paying RM5,000 off the loan restores 30%.

## X3 · Extension · CFA · Apply
An investor buys 1,000 shares at RM20 with 50% initial margin, pays RM600 interest, receives RM400 dividends, and sells at RM23. What is the return on invested capital?

A. 15.0%
B. 30.0%
C. 28.0%
D. 34.0%

Answer: C
Why: Return = (income − interest + sale value − purchase value) / initial equity = (400 − 600 + 23,000 − 20,000) / 10,000 = 2,800 / 10,000 = **28%**. Without interest it would be 34% (D).

## X4 · Extension · CFA · Analyze
In a short sale (Table 2.6 logic), the investor sold 100 shares at RM50 and deposited RM2,500. At what share price does short-account margin fall to 30%?

## X5 · Extension · Case competition · Analyze
A popular stock is heavily shorted, then jumps on good news, and short sellers rush to buy shares to close their positions. What is the likely effect?

A. The price falls, because short sellers are selling.
B. No effect, because shorts are closed off-exchange.
C. Their buying adds demand and can push the price up further: a "short squeeze" that magnifies short sellers' losses.
D. Margin calls only affect long investors.

Answer: C
Why: Closing a short requires buying. Forced buying from rising losses and margin calls adds demand, an extension of the chapter's point that a rising price hurts short sellers, and can feed on itself.

## X6 · Extension · Case competition · Evaluate

## X9 · Extension · PhD · Analyze
Leverage of 2× (50% margin) doubles the expected excess return on equity. What does it do to the standard deviation of equity returns, ignoring interest-rate changes?

A. Roughly doubles it, so the risk-return trade-off per unit of risk is not improved.
B. Leaves it unchanged.
C. Halves it.

Answer: A
Why: Scaling a position by 2 scales both its mean excess return and its volatility by about 2. Leverage magnifies; it does not create a better risk-return ratio, and borrowing costs make it slightly worse.

## X10 · Extension · PhD · Evaluate
