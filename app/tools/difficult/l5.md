# Chapter 5 — Difficult mode

## D1 · Remember
How does the chapter define an **efficient portfolio**?

A. The portfolio with the highest possible return, whatever the risk.
B. The portfolio that provides the highest return for a given level of risk.
C. The portfolio with the largest number of securities.
D. The portfolio with zero standard deviation.

Answer: B
Why: Efficiency is about the trade-off: highest return for a given risk (equivalently, lowest risk for a given return). A ignores risk, C confuses count with quality, and D is only possible in special cases.

## D2 · Remember
What is the beta of the market portfolio?

A. 1.0
B. 0.5
C. 0
D. 2.0

Answer: A
Why: Beta measures sensitivity to the market benchmark, so the market's own beta is 1.0. A beta of 0 means no linear sensitivity to the market.

## D3 · Remember
Which risk can be reduced or eliminated through diversification?

A. Unsystematic (diversifiable, firm-specific) risk
B. Systematic (nondiversifiable) risk
C. Inflation risk affecting all firms
D. War and political risk

Answer: A
Why: Firm-specific events such as lawsuits and strikes can be diluted by holding many securities. Systematic risk arises from forces that affect similar investments broadly, so diversification cannot remove it.

## D4 · Understand
Why is the standard deviation of a portfolio generally **not** the weighted average of its assets' standard deviations?

A. Because standard deviations cannot be averaged by law.
B. Because standard deviation is always larger for portfolios.
C. Because weights do not affect risk.
D. Because portfolio risk also depends on the correlations between the assets: imperfect co-movement lets fluctuations partly offset.

Answer: D
Why: The covariance term 2w<sub>A</sub>w<sub>B</sub>σ<sub>A</sub>σ<sub>B</sub>ρ explains it. Only when ρ = +1 does the portfolio σ equal the weighted average.

## D5 · Understand
Which statement best contrasts beta and standard deviation?

A. They measure exactly the same thing.
B. Both measure only firm-specific risk.
C. Beta measures total volatility; standard deviation measures market sensitivity.
D. Standard deviation measures total volatility; beta measures sensitivity to market movements (systematic risk).

Answer: D
Why: A stock driven by company-specific news may have a high σ but a low β. Neither replaces the other for every purpose.

## D6 · Understand
Why does CAPM reward only systematic risk?

A. Because unsystematic risk does not exist.
B. Because CAPM was designed for bonds only.
C. Because systematic risk is always smaller.
D. Because investors can cheaply diversify away firm-specific risk, so the market has no reason to compensate them for holding it.

Answer: D
Why: More total risk does not automatically justify a higher CAPM return. What matters is the asset's contribution to market-related risk (an assumption-driven theoretical result).

## D7 · Apply
A portfolio is 40% in an asset with E(R) = 10% and 60% in an asset with E(R) = 5%. What is the portfolio's expected return?

A. 7.5%
B. 6.0%
C. 7.0%
D. 15.0%

Answer: C
Why: E(R<sub>p</sub>) = 0.40(10%) + 0.60(5%) = 4% + 3% = **7%**. A is the simple average; D is the sum.

## D8 · Apply
R<sub>f</sub> = 5%, E(R<sub>m</sub>) = 11% and β = 1.2. What is the CAPM required return?

A. 12.2%
B. 18.2%
C. 13.2%
D. 7.2%

Answer: A
Why: R = 5% + 1.2 × (11% − 5%) = 5% + 7.2% = **12.2%**. B wrongly multiplies beta by the market return; D adds only the premium part.

## D9 · Apply
A portfolio holds 50% in a stock with β = 1.2, 30% with β = 0.8 and 20% with β = 1.5. What is the portfolio beta?

A. 1.14
B. 1.00
C. 1.17
D. 1.50

Answer: A
Why: β<sub>p</sub> = 0.5(1.2) + 0.3(0.8) + 0.2(1.5) = 0.60 + 0.24 + 0.30 = **1.14**. Portfolio beta is the weighted average of the betas.

## D10 · Analyze
Two assets have σ = 20% and 10% and are held 50/50. If they are uncorrelated (ρ = 0), what is the portfolio standard deviation?

A. 15.00%
B. 8.66%
C. 11.18%
D. 5.00%

Answer: C
Why: Variance = 0.25(0.04) + 0.25(0.01) = 0.0125; σ<sub>p</sub> = √0.0125 ≈ **11.18%**. A is the weighted average (ρ = +1); B is ρ = −0.5; D is ρ = −1.

## D11 · Analyze
A stock has σ = 25%, a correlation of 0.6 with the market, and the market has σ = 15%. What is its beta?

A. 0.40
B. 0.60
C. 1.67
D. 1.00

Answer: D
Why: β = ρ(σ<sub>i</sub> / σ<sub>m</sub>) = 0.6 × (25 / 15) = **1.0**. You cannot get beta from the stock's σ alone.

## D12 · Analyze
Stock A has β = 0.5 and σ = 45%. Stock B has β = 1.5 and σ = 30%. Which statement is correct?

A. A is safer on every measure.
B. B has both higher beta and higher total volatility.
C. A has lower market sensitivity but higher total volatility; neither is unconditionally safer.
D. Beta and σ must always rank assets identically.

Answer: C
Why: Beta captures systematic exposure; σ captures total variation including firm-specific risk. A low beta does not make a stock low-volatility overall.

## D13 · Evaluate
A stock with β = 1.5 is expected by an analyst to return 15%. R<sub>f</sub> = 4% and E(R<sub>m</sub>) = 10%. Evaluate.

A. It is overvalued: below the SML.
B. Required return is 13%, so expected alpha is +2%: above the SML, potentially undervalued, but only as reliable as the forecast.
C. It is fairly priced exactly on the SML.
D. CAPM cannot be applied with β above 1.

Answer: B
Why: Required = 4% + 1.5(6%) = 13%. Positive alpha is not proof of a bargain; it may reflect an inaccurate forecast or an inadequate model.

## D14 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "Adding more stocks to a portfolio always reduces risk."

A. Incorrect: a highly correlated additional stock adds little diversification; what matters is whether the new asset reduces covariance or concentration.
B. Correct.
C. Correct, but only for stocks above beta 1.
D. Incorrect, because adding stocks always increases risk.

Answer: A
Why: Number of assets ≠ quality of diversification. Forty bank stocks are still one big bet on banking.

## D15 · Evaluate
Evaluate: "I'll choose a portfolio below the efficient frontier because it feels safer."

A. Sensible: feelings determine efficiency.
B. Flawed: a dominated portfolio offers the same risk for less return; a cautious investor should pick a lower-risk point on the frontier instead.
C. Correct, because the frontier is always riskier.
D. Correct, because portfolios below the frontier have zero risk.

Answer: B
Why: Comfort and efficiency are different things. Risk preference selects among efficient portfolios; it is not a reason to hold an inefficient one.

## D16 · Create
Asset A has σ = 30% and asset B has σ = 10%, with ρ = −1. Which weights make the portfolio riskless?

A. 50% A, 50% B
B. 75% A, 25% B
C. 25% A, 75% B
D. 10% A, 90% B

Answer: C
Why: Set w<sub>A</sub>σ<sub>A</sub> = w<sub>B</sub>σ<sub>B</sub>: w<sub>A</sub> × 30 = (1 − w<sub>A</sub>) × 10, so w<sub>A</sub> = 10 / 40 = **25%**. Check: 0.25 × 30 = 7.5 = 0.75 × 10.

## D17 · Create
Stock X has β = 1.4 and Stock Y has β = 0.6. What weight in X gives a two-stock portfolio with β = 1.0?

A. 25%
B. 60%
C. 50%
D. 75%

Answer: C
Why: w(1.4) + (1 − w)(0.6) = 1.0 → 0.8w = 0.4 → **w = 0.5**. Beta is linear in the weights.

## D18 · Create
Which sequence best constructs a portfolio for an investor, per the chapter's reconciliation of traditional and modern approaches?

Why: This reproduces the chapter's policy: traditional diversification and judgment plus MPT's statistical discipline (correlations and beta).

## X1 · Extension · Industry · Apply
R<sub>f</sub> = 4% and E(R<sub>m</sub>) = 10%. What does CAPM require from an asset with β = −0.5, and why is that sensible?

A. 7%, because all assets need more than the market.
B. 4%, because beta never changes required return.
C. −4%, because it must lose money.
D. 1%: a negative-beta asset tends to rise when the market falls, so it acts as a hedge and investors accept less return for it.

Answer: D
Why: 4% + (−0.5)(6%) = 1%. Asset value inside a portfolio depends on how it co-moves with everything else, not only on its own expected return.

## X2 · Extension · Industry · Understand
Why do index-fund providers emphasise holding the whole market rather than a few stocks, in terms of the chapter's risk decomposition?

Why: The diversification curve (Figure 5.8) flattens at the systematic-risk floor. Holding more securities beyond that gives diminishing benefit.

## X3 · Extension · CFA · Apply
w<sub>A</sub> = 0.6, w<sub>B</sub> = 0.4, σ<sub>A</sub> = 15%, σ<sub>B</sub> = 25%, ρ = 0.2. What is σ<sub>p</sub>?

A. 19.0%
B. 14.7%
C. 12.0%
D. 21.7%

Answer: B
Why: Variance = 0.36(0.0225) + 0.16(0.0625) + 2(0.6)(0.4)(0.15)(0.25)(0.2) = 0.0081 + 0.0100 + 0.0036 = 0.0217; σ<sub>p</sub> = √0.0217 ≈ **14.7%**. A is the weighted average of the σs.

## X4 · Extension · CFA · Analyze
A security has E(R) = 9%, β = 0.7, R<sub>f</sub> = 4% and E(R<sub>m</sub>) = 10%. What is its expected alpha?

A. −0.8%
B. 0%
C. +5.0%
D. +0.8%

Answer: D
Why: Required = 4% + 0.7(6%) = 8.2%. Alpha = 9% − 8.2% = **+0.8%**: above the SML.

## X5 · Extension · Case competition · Analyze
During a global crisis the correlation between two assets (σ = 20% and 10%, 50/50) rises from 0 to 0.8. Approximately what happens to σ<sub>p</sub>?

A. It falls from 11.2% to 8%.
B. It becomes 5%.
C. It stays at 11.2%.
D. It rises from about 11.2% to about 14.3%.

Answer: D
Why: Variance = 0.0125 + 2(0.25)(0.2)(0.1)ρ = 0.0125 + 0.01ρ. At ρ = 0.8: 0.0205, so σ<sub>p</sub> ≈ 14.3%. Historical low correlation is not guaranteed future low correlation.

## X6 · Extension · Case competition · Evaluate
Portfolios A, B and C have expected alphas of +0.8%, +1.4% and +1.0%, and standard deviations of 12%, 18% and 28%. Evaluate "Portfolio B must be chosen because it has the highest alpha."

## X7 · Extension · Master's · Analyze
Why can estimated betas be unstable?

A. They are estimated from historical returns over a particular window and benchmark, so changes in the firm, the market or the estimation period alter the estimate.
B. Betas are set by regulators each quarter.
C. Beta is always exactly 1.
D. Beta depends only on dividends.

Answer: A
Why: Beta describes a linear relationship estimated from past data; it is not a mechanical multiplier. This limits how confidently SML-based conclusions can be drawn.

## X8 · Extension · Master's · Evaluate
Portfolio W (β = 0.91) is less market-sensitive than V (β = 1.195). Evaluate: "W therefore has lower total standard deviation."

## X9 · Extension · PhD · Analyze
A stock's firm-specific risk doubles, but its covariance with the market is unchanged. What does CAPM predict for its required return?

A. It doubles.
B. It is unchanged: beta depends on covariance with the market, and unsystematic risk is not priced.
C. It falls to the risk-free rate.
D. It becomes negative.

Answer: B
Why: β = Cov(R<sub>i</sub>, R<sub>m</sub>) / Var(R<sub>m</sub>). Unchanged covariance means unchanged beta, so the same required return. Investors could diversify the extra risk away.

## X10 · Extension · PhD · Evaluate
An optimiser built from historical estimates recommends a very concentrated "efficient" portfolio. What is the best critical response?

Why: The chapter warns that MPT depends on statistical estimates, so a mathematically optimal portfolio can perform poorly in reality if the estimates are poor.
