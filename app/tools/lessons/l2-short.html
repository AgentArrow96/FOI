THE BIG STORY

Sell high first, buy low later

A short seller sells shares they do not own, borrowed through a broker from other investors' street-name holdings, hoping to buy them back more cheaply and return them. It reverses the usual order: borrow → sell → buy back → return. It looks like the mirror image of a long purchase, but the risk is not symmetrical.

{{vis:l2_short_steps}}

The chapter's example

Profit = sale proceeds − cost to buy back

Borrow and sell 100 shares at RM50: +RM5,000. Buy back at RM30: −RM3,000. Return the shares. Profit before costs: RM2,000.

The loss has no ceiling

At RM100 the loss is RM50 a share; at RM200 it is RM150. There is no fixed upper limit to the price.

The maximum gain is limited (the price cannot fall below zero: RM50 a share here), but the potential loss is theoretically unlimited. And you still owe the shares while the price runs against you.

Short sales also need margin

Account equity = funds held by the broker − current cost to buy back the shares. Margin = equity / current buy-back cost.

Table 2.6: proceeds RM5,000 + RM2,500 deposit = RM7,500 held. At RM70 the buy-back costs RM7,000, equity is RM500 and margin is 500 / 7,000 ≈ 7.1%, below 30%: margin call.

Advantage

  • A chance to profit when a stock's price declines

Disadvantages

  • Limited return: the price can't go below zero
  • Unlimited risk: the price can rise without limit
  • Must still buy back and return the borrowed shares
  • Short sellers do not earn dividends

ANALYSE

The same 20% price move, three investors

Cash long, 50%-margin long and short, ignoring costs.

If the price rises 20%: cash long +20%, margin long about +40% on equity, short loses. If it falls 20%, the signs flip. Market direction + position structure = investor outcome.
TRAP

"Shorting is just the mirror image of buying, so the risks are the same."

True or false?

False. A long position can lose at most what was invested (the price stops at zero); a short position's loss has no ceiling because the price can keep rising.

Exam lens

  • Sequence: borrow → sell → buy back → return. Profit = (sale price − buy-back price) × shares.
  • Gain capped, loss unlimited: the asymmetry the chapter stresses.
  • In a short account a rising price shrinks equity and can trigger a margin call.

Quick check

Why can a long position never lose more than 100% of the cash invested, but a short position can?

A share price cannot fall below zero, which caps the long loss. A short seller must buy back at whatever the price becomes, and there is no upper limit to a price.
