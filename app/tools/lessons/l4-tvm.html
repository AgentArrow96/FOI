THE BIG STORY

RM100 today or RM100 next year?

If you can earn 8%, RM100 today becomes RM108 in a year, so the two are not equivalent. Whenever money can earn interest, its value depends on when it is received: the time value of money. Interest is the "rent" a borrower pays for using a lender's money. Everything in Appendix 4A follows from that one idea: money has an amount and a date.

Simple interest

Interest is paid only on the initial deposit.

  • RM1,000 at 10% for 3 years: 100 + 100 + 100
  • FV = RM1,300

Compound interest

Interest is paid on the deposit and on interest already earned.

  • 1,000 → 1,100 → 1,210 → 1,331
  • FV = RM1,331; the extra RM31 is interest on interest

Two formulas, one equation run in both directions

FV: how much will it grow to?

$FV = PV(1+r)^n$: each period multiplies the money by $(1+r)$; the exponent counts how many times it has been allowed to grow.

Chapter example: RM1,000 at 8% for 2 years: $1{,}000(1.08)^2 = 1{,}166.40$.

PV: what is a future amount worth today?

Divide both sides by $(1+r)^n$: $PV = \dfrac{FV}{(1+r)^n}$. Discounting is compounding run backwards.

Chapter example: RM1,000 in 7 years at 8%: $1{,}000 / 1.08^7 \approx 583.49$. So RM583.49 today is economically equivalent to RM1,000 in seven years at 8%.

How r and n move the answers

$r\uparrow$ or $n\uparrow$ ⇒ $FV\uparrow$. $r\uparrow$ or $n\uparrow$ ⇒ $PV\downarrow$.

RM1,000 due in a year is worth RM952.38 at 5% but RM833.33 at 20%; due in ten years at 10% it is worth only about RM385.54. A higher opportunity rate means you need less today to reach the same future sum.

Calculator keys and the sign convention

N = number of periods · I = interest rate per period · PV · PMT (payment, for annuities only) · FV · CPT computes the unknown.

Money paid out is negative and money received is positive. That is cash-flow direction, not a "negative investment".

The most important exam rule

If cash flows occur at different times, do not compare them directly. Move them all to the same date first: all to present value, or all to future value.

Value today = present value of the future benefits (the foundation of bond, stock and project valuation)

ANALYSE

RM1,000 today or RM1,200 in 3 years, at 8%?

Which is worth more?

PV of RM1,200 = 1,200 / 1.08³ ≈ RM952.60 < RM1,000. Take the RM1,000 today: the bigger nominal number loses once both are at the same date.
EVALUATE

"Compound interest only matters at high interest rates."

Agree?

No. It matters whenever r > 0 and n > 1. Growth is exponential, $(1+r)^n$, so small differences become large over long horizons.
APPLY

RM5,000 invested for 6 years at 7%

Which formula, and is there a PMT?

A single lump sum moving forward: $FV = 5{,}000(1.07)^6 \approx$ RM7,503.65. PMT = 0; not every TVM problem is an annuity.

Exam lens

  • Simple interest: on the original amount only. Compound: on principal plus accumulated interest.
  • FV moves money forward, PV moves it back: inverse operations.
  • Before using any formula ask: one cash flow or many? Forward or backward in time?

Quick check

Which is worth more today at 10%: RM50,000 now or RM75,000 in five years?

PV of RM75,000 = 75,000 / 1.10⁵ ≈ RM46,569 < RM50,000. The RM50,000 now is financially superior at that rate.
