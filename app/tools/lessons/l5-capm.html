THE BIG STORY

Why should the market pay you for risk you could have diversified away for free?

If investors can cheaply remove a company's firm-specific risk by holding a broad portfolio, the market has no reason to keep rewarding anyone who chooses not to. Under the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM), only systematic risk, measured by beta, earns a higher required return. More total risk does not automatically justify a higher CAPM return.

The CAPM equation

A model linking risk and return to define the required return on an investment, using beta, the risk-free rate and the expected market return. $E(R_m) - R_f$ is the market risk premium; beta scales it.

Required return = R_f + β × [E(R_m) − R_f]

Worked examples (Rf = 4%, E(Rm) = 10%, premium 6%)

1.5 times the market's sensitivity, 1.5 times its premium

$R = 4\% + 1.5(10\% - 4\%) = 4\% + 9\% = 13\%$

Required return rises with beta

β 0.5 → 7%; β 1.0 → 10% (the market itself); β 1.5 → 13%.

The risk-free rate is the baseline; beta determines the extra compensation.

Beta zero or negative

β = 0 → R = 4%, the risk-free rate. β = −0.5 → R = 4% − 3% = 1%.

A negative-beta asset tends to rise when the market falls: a hedge. Investors may accept a lower expected return for that protection.

The Security Market Line: CAPM as a graph

Plot required return against beta for many betas and you get the SML. In the chapter's Figure 5.6: Rf = 6%, required return at β = 1 is 10%, so $R_i = 6\% + 4\%\beta_i$ and β = 1.25 requires 11%.

InterceptThe risk-free rate: the return required at zero beta.
SlopeThe market risk premium: extra required return per unit of beta.
Above the SMLExpected return exceeds the CAPM requirement: positive expected alpha, potentially undervalued.
Below the SMLExpected return falls short: negative alpha, potentially overvalued.

Alpha = expected return − CAPM-required return. A positive alpha may just mean the forecast or the model is wrong; it is not proof of a bargain.

Required return?

DECIDE

β = 1.5 with Rf 4%, E(Rm) 10%; your forecast is 15%

Above or below the SML?

Required 13%, forecast 15%: alpha +2%, above the SML and potentially attractive if the forecast is reliable.
EVALUATE

"Buy the highest-beta stock to earn the highest return."

Agree?

No. CAPM links beta to required expected return, not guaranteed realised return. A β 2.0 stock can lose 40% in a bad year; high beta magnifies market falls too.

Exam lens

  • CAPM: R = Rf + β[E(Rm) − Rf]; beta multiplies the premium, not the market return.
  • SML: required return vs beta. Efficient frontier: expected return vs σp. Don't swap the axes.
  • Only nondiversifiable risk has a positive risk-return relationship under CAPM.

Quick check

A common mistake computes CAPM as Rf + β × E(Rm). With Rf 5%, E(Rm) 11% and β = 1.2, compare the wrong and right answers.

Wrong: 5% + 1.2 × 11% = 18.2%. Right: 5% + 1.2 × (11% − 5%) = 12.2%. Beta multiplies the market risk premium.
