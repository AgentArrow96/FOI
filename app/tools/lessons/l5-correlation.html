THE BIG STORY

Asset A earns 20% in booms and 0% in slumps. Asset B does the opposite.

Hold 50% of each and you earn 10% in both scenarios. Neither asset became safer; their fluctuations offset each other. Correlation, the statistical relationship between two series of returns, is the reason diversification works.

{{vis:l5_offset}}

The correlation coefficient, ρ, from −1 to +1

ρ = +1: move together exactly

Positively correlated items move in the same direction. At +1, combining them eliminates no risk.

ρ ≈ 0: no relationship

Uncorrelated series lack any relationship; combining them can still reduce risk substantially.

ρ = −1: move opposite exactly

The slides say this "eliminates all risk": true only with the right weights.

With σA = 20% and σB = 10%, a 50/50 mix still has 5% volatility. Zero needs $w_A\sigma_A = w_B\sigma_B$: wA = 1/3, wB = 2/3.

The lower the correlation, the lower the portfolio risk, with individual volatilities unchanged. Figure 5.4 shows the risk-return combinations bending further left as ρ falls.

The X, Y and Z example (Table 5.3)

X: 8, 10, 12, 14, 16%. Y: 24, 20, 16, 12, 8%. Z: 8, 12, 16, 20, 24%.

  • ⅔X + ⅓Y earns 13.33% every year: σ = 0%
  • ⅔X + ⅓Z also averages 13.33% but σ = 4.2%
  • XY dominates XZ: same return, less risk

International diversification

  • Foreign economic cycles may not move with the home cycle: a new source of low correlation
  • Methods: foreign stocks on home exchanges, Yankee bonds, ADRs, mutual funds investing abroad
  • Costs: currency risk, less convenience, higher expense, often riskier markets
  • In a global crisis correlations can jump: historical low correlation ≠ guaranteed future low correlation

ANALYSE

Two investments each have σ = 15%

Can an equal-weighted portfolio have σ below 15%?

Yes, whenever ρ < +1. Correlation decides how big the diversification benefit is.
EVALUATE

"Stock A is far more volatile than B, so adding A must raise my portfolio's risk."

Agree?

Not necessarily. What matters is A's correlation with the existing holdings and its weight. Individual asset risk ≠ marginal contribution to portfolio risk.
EVALUATE

"Adding more stocks always reduces risk."

Agree?

No. Another highly correlated stock adds little diversification. Ask whether the new asset reduces concentration or covariance, not how many stocks you now own.

Exam lens

  • ρ ranges from −1 to +1; the lower it is, the greater the potential risk reduction.
  • ρ = +1: no reduction. ρ = −1: zero risk is possible, but only with the right weights.
  • Correlation describes co-movement, not profitability.

Quick check

σA = 30%, σB = 10%, ρ = −1. What weight in A makes the portfolio riskless?

Set wA × 30% = (1 − wA) × 10%: wA = 10/40 = 25%, with 75% in B.
