THE BIG STORY

Your portfolio earned 8%. Good or bad?

Impossible to say in isolation. If the market fell 10%, 8% is excellent; if it rose 25%, 8% is poor. Performance must be judged against a relevant benchmark, and market averages and indexes exist to provide one. They compress hundreds of stock prices into a single number.

1Gauge conditionsIs the market broadly rising or falling?
2BenchmarkCompare your portfolio with a large, diversified one.
3Study cyclesTrends and behaviour over time.
4Average vs indexAverages track arithmetic price behaviour; indexes measure current value relative to a base period.

The main indicators

Dow Jones Industrial Average

The most popular average: 30 high-quality, diversified stocks; its make-up changes over time.

Also: Dow Jones Transportation (20 stocks) and Utilities (15) averages.

Standard & Poor's 500

500 stocks from major industries: broader and more representative of the overall market than the DJIA. A true index, with a 1941–43 base period.

Different baskets measure different things

NYSE and AMEX composites; Nasdaq Composite (often a technology barometer); Value Line Composite (equal-weighted); Wilshire 5000; Russell 1000 (large firms) and 2000 (small firms); EAFE MSCI (foreign markets).

Bond yield

The total return from buying a bond at its current price and holding it to maturity: interest plus or minus the change in the bond's value, as an annual rate.

Coupon ≠ yield: buying below or above face value changes the return.

An index is not neutral

How an index is built changes what it measures. In a value-weighted index the biggest company dominates; in a price-weighted average the highest-priced share dominates; in an equal-weighted index (Value Line) every stock counts the same. The same market can therefore produce different "market returns".

COMPARE

Which better represents the broad U.S. market, the DJIA or the S&P 500?

Why?

The S&P 500: 500 companies across major industries versus the DJIA's 30 stocks.
EVALUATE

"The index rose, so my portfolio should have too."

Agree or disagree?

Not necessarily. The portfolio may differ in sector, company size, geography, asset type and weighting. The real question: is the benchmark representative of my portfolio?
APPLY

A Malaysian small-cap fund returned 12%; the S&P 500 returned 18%

Did the fund underperform?

You can't tell from this comparison: the S&P 500 is the wrong benchmark. Compare with a small-cap index matching the fund's market and size.

Exam lens

  • Indexes are used to gauge market conditions, benchmark performance and study cycles.
  • DJIA = 30 stocks; S&P 500 = broader and more representative.
  • Benchmark selection must match the exposure being evaluated; two broad indexes move similarly but not identically.

Quick check

Why can the DJIA and the S&P 500 produce different returns over the same period?

They contain different stocks and use different weighting rules, so the same market environment does not produce identical index returns.
