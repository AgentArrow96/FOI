THE BIG STORY

A mansion worth RM10 million is not liquid

If you need cash tomorrow, selling it may require a huge discount. The chapter defines liquidity as the ability to convert an investment into cash quickly and with little or no loss in value: two conditions, speed and price. Short-term investments are bought mainly for that liquidity, and that is why they pay so little.

Advantages of short-term investments

  • High liquidity
  • Low risk of default

Disadvantages

  • Low levels of return
  • Loss of purchasing power from inflation

Desirable features cost return

Investors value safety and liquidity. If an asset already gives you both, why would its issuer also need to pay a high return? That intuition underlies much of asset pricing: the more desirable features an asset has, the less compensation investors require.

More desirable features → less compensation required

"Safe" has more than one meaning

Your savings account earns 2% while inflation is 4%. Your balance grows, but approximately 2% − 4% = −2% of real growth: purchasing power falls.

Default risk: cash is very safe

Little chance of not getting your ringgit back.

This is nominal safety: "I didn't lose ringgit."

Short-term price volatility: very low

The account balance does not swing from day to day.

Good for money you will need soon.

Liquidity: excellent

Fast conversion to cash with little price sacrifice.

Often liquidity is the service being bought.

Long-term purchasing power: not guaranteed

If the return is below inflation, real wealth shrinks.

This is real economic safety: "I didn't lose purchasing power." So: safety is multidimensional.

What short-term investments are for

Emergency reserve

The step-1 prerequisite: cash available without a forced sale.

Short-term goals

Saving for a specific near-term expense.

As an investment

Part of a diversified portfolio, or a temporary home while waiting for better opportunities. Here yield matters as much as safety.

The slide on investment suitability separates savings use (safety and security first) from investment use (yield matters as much as safety).

The short-term spectrum

Savings accounts, NOW and money market deposit accounts, asset management accounts, U.S. Treasury bills and I bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, banker's acceptances, money-market mutual funds. The slide 38 scorecard rates them on availability, safety, liquidity and rate, and no instrument wins on every dimension. That trade-off, not "instrument X is best", is the lesson.

EVALUATE

"Cash is the safest investment."

Agree or disagree?

It depends on which safety. Default risk, volatility and liquidity: very safe. Long-term purchasing power: not necessarily, because inflation can erode real wealth. Safety is multidimensional.
ANALYSE

Why would anyone buy T-bills that return less than stocks?

Name the considerations.

Horizon, liquidity, low default risk, low volatility and emergency needs. For money needed soon, these features are worth more than a higher but uncertain return.
TRAP

"Higher liquidity always makes an investment superior."

True or false?

False. Liquidity is valuable but has a price: lower return. For a 30-year retirement goal, paying for liquidity you will never use can be a poor trade-off.

Exam lens

  • Liquidity = fast conversion and little or no loss of value.
  • Short-term investments: high liquidity, low default risk; but low returns and inflation risk.
  • Market liquidity ≠ price stability: a share can be sold tomorrow at a much lower price.
  • There is no universally superior investment, only one that is more or less suitable for a particular objective.

Quick check

A deposit earns 3% while inflation is 5%. What is the approximate real return, and which disadvantage of short-term investments does this illustrate?

About 3% − 5% = −2%: the balance rises but purchasing power falls, the "loss of purchasing power from inflation" disadvantage.
