THE BIG STORY

A broker's core job is execution, not guaranteeing profits

Stockbrokers act as intermediaries between buyers and sellers, execute clients' orders, are usually paid by commission, and must be licensed. They may add research and statements. The investor's two decisions are how much service to pay for and what kind of account to open.

Three kinds of brokerage firm

Full-service broker

A broad range of services and products, research and advice; higher fees.

Worth paying for if you genuinely need advice, planning and research.

Premium discount broker

Low commissions with limited research and advice.

Basic discount broker

Mainly electronic execution, no research or advice, deep-discount commissions.

If you already know what you want to buy, paying for unused advice makes little sense. Ask: what am I paying for?

Street name

Securities are registered in the brokerage firm's name but held in trust for the client, who is the beneficial owner.

  • Legal registration (convenience) ≠ economic ownership

Churning: an incentive problem

Excessive trading in a client's account to generate commissions.

  • More trades → more commissions for the broker
  • More trades are not necessarily better for the client
  • Broker incentive ≠ investor objective unless aligned

Four types of brokerage account

Ask: who controls the account, and where does the money come from?

Cash accountThe investor can only make cash transactions: you fund every purchase yourself.
Margin accountThe broker extends borrowing privileges (Chapter 2's leverage).
Custodial accountFor a minor; a parent or guardian handles the transactions.
Wrap accountA professional manager makes the investment decisions for a flat annual fee.

Selecting a stockbroker, per the slides

Find someone who understands your investment goals; consider the broker's style and goals; be prepared to pay higher fees for full service; ask friends and associates for referrals; beware of churning.

APPLY

An experienced investor who only needs cheap execution

Which firm type?

A basic (or premium) discount broker: paying full-service fees for advice they won't use is wasteful.
CLASSIFY

A manager runs your portfolio for 1.5% a year, all trading included

Which account type?

A wrap account: decisions shift to a professional manager for a flat annual fee.
SPOT THE PROBLEM

A broker trades a retiree's account 40 times a month

What is this and why is it harmful?

Possible churning: excessive trading that generates commissions for the broker while costs eat into the client's returns.

Exam lens

  • Full-service vs premium discount vs basic discount: services versus cost.
  • Cash, margin, custodial and wrap accounts: who funds and who decides.
  • Street name: the broker is the registered holder, the client the beneficial owner.

Quick check

Your shares are held in street name and the broker's name is on the register. Who owns the economic interest?

You, the client: the broker holds the securities in trust for you as beneficial owner.
