THE BIG STORY

A stock trades around RM10 and you want to buy

With a market order you might pay RM10.02, or RM10.10, depending on liquidity. With a limit order at RM9.80 you pay RM9.80 or less, or you never trade at all. Every order trades off execution certainty against price control; you rarely get both. The right question is: which risk do I care about more?

The order types

Market order: execution first

Buy or sell at the best price available when the order is placed: the fastest way to fill an order.

You control timing, not the exact price.

Limit order: price first

Buy at or below a specified price, or sell at or above it. If the limit is not met, the order is not filled.

Price protection, at the cost of execution uncertainty.

Stop-loss: a trigger, not a guarantee

A "suspended" order to sell if the price reaches or falls below a set level; once activated it becomes a market order. Can be a day order or GTC.

If the stock gaps from RM18 to RM14 past a RM17 stop, you may sell near RM14. A buy stop above the price can limit losses on a short position.

Stop-limit: trigger plus price control

When the stop is reached, the order becomes a limit order: it prevents a sale at an undesirable price, but may not fill if prices keep falling.

How long an order lives

Fill-or-kill: execute immediately or cancel. Day order: expires at the end of the trading day if unfilled. Good-'til-canceled: stays in effect (the slides say up to six months) unless filled, cancelled or renewed.

Price condition + time condition = a complete order.

Round lots and odd lots

Round lot: 100 shares or multiples of 100. Odd lot: fewer than 100 shares (the textbook's convention).

Day trading: activity is not skill

Day traders buy and sell rapidly within the day for quick profits; the slides call it highly risky, especially with margin, and costly because of frequent commissions. More decisions feel like more control, but more trades mean more costs and more chances for error.

APPLY

Buy a thinly traded stock, but never above RM5.20

Which order?

A limit buy at RM5.20: a market order prioritises execution and could fill above the constraint.
EVALUATE

"A stop-loss at RM17 guarantees I lose no more than RM3 from RM20."

True?

False. The stop price is a trigger; the order then becomes a market order, so a downward gap can fill well below RM17. A stop-limit controls price but may not fill at all.
ANALYSE

Investor A must exit today; Investor B only buys if cheap

Who uses which order?

A values execution certainty → market order. B values price certainty → limit order. Same market, different objectives: suitability again.

Exam lens

  • Market = execution priority; limit = price priority.
  • Stop → market order when triggered; stop-limit → limit order when triggered.
  • Long positions use sell stops below the price; short positions use buy stops above it.

Quick check

You short a stock at RM50 and fear a sharp rise. What order protects you?

A buy stop above the current price, for example at RM60: if the price reaches RM60, a buy order closes the short.
