THE BIG STORY

RM1,000 at the end of every year for 8 years, at 8%

You deposit RM8,000 in total but end with RM10,636.63, because early deposits earn interest for longer. An annuity is a stream of equal cash flows at equal intervals; an ordinary annuity pays at the end of each period. When the cash flows are unequal, it is a mixed stream and there is no shortcut.

Annuity formulas, and why they work

Add up each deposit's future value

$FV_{\text{annuity}} = PMT\left[\dfrac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}\right]$, a compressed form of $PMT(1+r)^{n-1} + \dots + PMT$.

Three RM1,000 deposits at 10%: 1,210 + 1,100 + 1,000 = RM3,310. The first deposit grows two years, the last one not at all.

What are repeated future payments worth today?

$PV_{\text{annuity}} = PMT\left[\dfrac{1-(1+r)^{-n}}{r}\right]$

Chapter example: 50 a year for 5 years at 8% has PV ≈ 199.64, although 250 is paid in total. Every payment is worth less today because it is delayed.

Mixed stream: discount each one

$PV = \displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}$: value = the sum of discounted future cash flows.

Chapter example: 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 at 8% → 27.78 + 34.29 + 39.69 + 44.10 + 47.64 = 193.51. Excel's NPV function does exactly this.

RM8,000 now ≠ RM1,000 a year for 8 years

Same total contributions, different timing.

The lump sum compounds for the full eight years, so it grows to more. Time can partly substitute for extra capital: earlier money works longer.

Compounding more often raises the true rate

With a stated rate $r$ compounded $m$ times a year for $t$ years: $FV = PV\left(1+\dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{mt}$; the true (effective) annual rate is $\left(1+\dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{m} - 1$.

CLASSIFY

100, 120, 140, 160 at year-ends

Annuity or mixed stream?

Mixed stream: equal intervals but unequal amounts, so the annuity shortcut does not apply. Discount each cash flow separately.
CREATE

RM100,000 needed in 10 years at 6%

How do you find the equal annual saving?

Set $100{,}000 = PMT\,[(1.06)^{10}-1]/0.06$ and solve: the factor is about 13.18, so PMT ≈ RM7,587 a year. TVM used to design a plan, not just compute a value.
EVALUATE

"Pick the account with the most frequent compounding."

Always right?

At the same stated rate it gives a higher effective rate, but fees, withdrawal restrictions, liquidity and credit risk can outweigh the small extra interest. Compare complete terms.

Exam lens

  • Annuity = equal amounts at equal intervals; ordinary annuity = paid at the end of each period (no cash flow at t₀).
  • Mixed stream → no shortcut: discount each cash flow.
  • More frequent compounding → higher effective rate; adjust both the rate (r/m) and the number of periods (mt).

Quick check

Why is an annuity due (payments at the start of each period) worth more than an ordinary annuity with the same payments?

Every payment arrives one period earlier, so each one compounds one extra period (or is discounted one period less): its value is the ordinary annuity's value × (1 + r).
