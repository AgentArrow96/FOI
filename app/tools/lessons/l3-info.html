THE BIG STORY

"ABC will double in three months. Institutions are secretly buying. Act now."

Today the problem is not finding information: screeners, quotes, charts, filings and news are a tap away. The problem is deciding which information matters, which source is trustworthy, and how to interpret it. More information can simply mean more noise, more confidence and more trading, without more knowledge.

Online investing: the gains

  • Easier research, and easier to act on it
  • Speed, access, lower barriers
  • Tools for planning, screening and tracking portfolios

The warnings in the slides

  • No live broker as a "safety net"
  • Be sceptical of "free" advice; know what you are buying and from whom
  • Watch frequent trading (costs, short-term taxes) and margin risk

Removing friction removes useful friction too

The old process: research → call the broker → discuss → trade. Today: see a video → tap "Buy". That is efficient only when the investor's decision quality is already high.

Descriptive vs analytical information

Descriptive = what happened?

Factual data on the past behaviour of the economy, the market, an industry, a company or an investment.

"Revenue grew 15% last year."

Analytical = what might happen, and what does it mean?

Current data combined with projections and recommendations about potential investments.

"Revenue growth should slow to 5% because demand is weakening." It contains interpretation, assumptions and a forecast.

Facts are not conclusions

Ask of every statement: which part is observed data, and which part is judgment?

"Trading volume rose 50%" is descriptive. "Institutions are secretly accumulating" is analytical, and speculative unless supported by evidence.

Defending yourself

Regulation FD

Critical company information must be disclosed to professionals and the public at the same time: no selective disclosure.

Company sources

Stockholder reports and the annual Form 10-K filing, plus company websites, subscription services and brokerage reports.

Pump-and-dump

Promoters buy cheap, hype the stock, sell into the rise and leave late buyers holding the losses.

Five questions from the slides: Is the seller licensed? Is the investment registered? How does the risk compare with the potential reward? Do I understand the investment? Where can I turn for help?

CLASSIFY

"The company reported RM2 billion revenue; its share price should reach RM12."

Which part is descriptive?

The revenue figure is descriptive (it reports what happened). The price target is analytical: a forecast built on assumptions and judgment.
ANALYSE

Audited annual report vs an anonymous forum post

What should you compare, beyond "A is better"?

Reliability, the source's incentives, whether the claims can be verified, and how much is descriptive fact versus analytical opinion.
APPLY

A social-media tip says a stock will double

Outline the steps.

Identify the source; separate descriptive from analytical claims; verify against primary evidence (filings, announcements); check the promoter's incentives (do they own it?); assess the downside if the claim is false.

Exam lens

  • Descriptive = past facts; analytical = current data + projections + recommendations.
  • Primary sources (filings, audited statements) generally deserve more trust than opinion or anonymous commentary.
  • Always ask what the source gains if you believe it.

Quick check

A company tells a select group of analysts about a profit warning before the public. Which rule does this violate, and why does it matter?

Regulation FD: material information must be disclosed to professionals and the public simultaneously. Selective disclosure gives a privileged group an unfair informational advantage.
