THE BIG STORY

You buy a company's new shares for RM10. Six months later you sell them to a friend for RM14.

Your RM10 financed the company. The friend's RM14 went to you, not the company. Those are two different markets, and the test that separates them is simple: follow the money. Chapter 2 is about the machinery that lets securities be created, sold, traded, priced, regulated, borrowed against and shorted.

Two separate questions, four labels

Money/capital and primary/secondary are independent dimensions, not four mutually exclusive boxes.

Money market: short-term securities

The market where short-term securities are bought and sold.

Think temporary financing and liquidity management. Forget the word "money": the distinction is about maturity.

Capital market: long-term securities

The market where long-term securities such as stocks and bonds are bought and sold.

Long-term capital formation. Common stock never matures, so it is always a capital-market instrument.

Primary market: new issues

The market in which new issues of securities are sold to the public.

The issuer raises new capital. Three ways to market a new issue: a public offering, a rights offering (to existing shareholders) or a private placement.

Secondary market: resale

The market in which securities are traded after they have been issued.

Existing investors trade among themselves; the issuer receives nothing from the resale.

"Secondary markets don't matter: the company gets no money"

A surface-level view that MCQs love to test.

Why they matter anyway

  • Liquidity: an exit route makes buying new issues attractive in the first place
  • Continuous pricing: trading discovers a market price every day
  • Primary and secondary markets perform different, complementary functions

Bull and bear markets: causes, not just directions

Bull marketFavourable markets, rising prices, investor optimism, economic growth or recovery, government stimulus.
Bear marketUnfavourable markets, falling prices, pessimism, economic slowdown, government restraint.

Better than "bull = up": economic expectations → expected corporate performance → investor expectations → demand for securities → prices. And the loop runs both ways: rising prices can feed optimism.

CLASSIFY

A corporation issues a new 10-year bond; you buy it at issuance

Which two labels?

Primary (newly issued) and capital market (10-year maturity). Classify both dimensions separately.
CLASSIFY

A government issues a 90-day Treasury bill and you buy it at issuance

Which two labels?

Primary money market. A government security is not automatically a long-term capital-market instrument.
FOLLOW THE MONEY

Investor A sells 1,000 existing ABC shares to Investor B on the exchange

Who receives the proceeds?

Investor A, the seller. It is a secondary-market trade; ABC raises no new capital from it.

Exam lens

  • Best single test: is the issuer raising new capital, or are existing investors trading among themselves?
  • Secondary markets provide liquidity and continuous pricing; the slides list both.
  • Always answer both questions: new or resale? short- or long-term?

Quick check

An existing 10-year bond is resold before maturity with 8 months left to run. Primary or secondary? Does the issuer receive the sale price?

Secondary market (a resale), so the issuer receives nothing; the selling investor does.
