The four-move answer (10–20 mark questions)

1. Establish the principle

Define the concept accurately in the chapter's terms: time value of money, total return, required return, HPR, IRR, risk, standard deviation, CV.

2. Explain the mechanism

Show why the relationship exists: the economic cause, not just the result.

3. Apply and analyse

Connect it to the scenario: calculate (FV, PV, HPR, IRR, σ, CV) with formula and substitution, then compare with the required return.

4. Evaluate and conclude

Weigh alternatives, name a limitation or assumption, and give a justified, conditional conclusion.

Chapter 4 + 4A answers combine calculation with judgment: a number is never the conclusion until it is compared with the required return or the right alternative.

Weak answer

Q: Evaluate whether a 15% expected return makes an investment attractive.

"A 15% return is attractive because it is a high return and would allow investors to earn more profit."

Treats a number as good in isolation; never mentions risk or required return.

Strong answer

"An investment's attractiveness cannot be judged from its expected return alone. Under the risk-return trade-off, the expected return must be compared with the required return, which combines the risk-free rate and a risk premium. If the required return is 18%, an expected 15% is insufficient compensation despite looking high; if it is 10%, the same investment may be acceptable. The conclusion also depends on how reliable the expected cash flows are and on the investor's ability to bear unfavourable outcomes. So 15% must be judged relative to its risk."

Principle + mechanism + application with numbers + conditional evaluation.
