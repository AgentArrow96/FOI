The four-move answer (10–20 mark questions)

1. Establish the principle

Define the concept accurately in the chapter's terms: portfolio return and σ, correlation, systematic vs unsystematic risk, beta, CAPM, SML, efficient frontier.

2. Explain the mechanism

Show why the relationship exists: the economic cause, not just the result.

3. Apply and analyse

Connect it to the scenario: compute E(Rp), σp, βp or the CAPM return, then interpret what it means for the whole portfolio.

4. Evaluate and conclude

Weigh alternatives, name a limitation or assumption, and give a justified, conditional conclusion.

Chapter 5 answers must move from the single asset to the portfolio: what matters is how an investment changes the risk and return of everything the investor already holds.

Weak answer

Q: Evaluate whether including a high-risk stock necessarily increases portfolio risk.

"Including a high-risk stock increases portfolio risk because its standard deviation is high. Investors should avoid risky stocks if they want a safe portfolio."

Confuses individual risk with the stock's contribution to portfolio risk.

Strong answer

"A stock's standard deviation measures the dispersion of its own returns, but its effect on portfolio risk also depends on its weight and its correlation with the existing holdings. Assets with less than perfectly positive correlation partly offset each other, so adding a volatile stock can lower total portfolio σ if the diversification benefit is large enough: in the chapter's IBM–Celgene example the combined portfolio is less volatile than IBM alone. Correlations and weights can change, though. The stock should be judged by its contribution to portfolio risk, not rejected for its standalone volatility."

Mechanism + textbook evidence + conditions + justified conclusion.
