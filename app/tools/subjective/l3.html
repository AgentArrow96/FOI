The four-move answer (10–20 mark questions)

1. Establish the principle

Define the concept accurately in the chapter's terms: descriptive vs analytical information, index, broker and account types, order types.

2. Explain the mechanism

Show why the relationship exists: the economic cause, not just the result.

3. Apply and analyse

Connect it to the scenario: the investor's objective (speed or price?), the reliability of the source, the right benchmark, the costs.

4. Evaluate and conclude

Weigh alternatives, name a limitation or assumption, and give a justified, conditional conclusion.

Chapter 3 rewards judgment: the best order, broker or source is the one that matches the investor's objective, and every choice has a trade-off.

Weak answer

Q: Evaluate the suitability of a stop-loss order for managing investment risk.

"A stop-loss order automatically sells shares when the price drops to a certain level. It helps investors avoid losses."

Overstates the protection: misses that the stop price is not guaranteed.

Strong answer

"A sell stop-loss order initiates a sale when the market price reaches a set trigger. Once activated it becomes a market order, helping the investor exit an unfavourable position, but the stop price does not guarantee the execution price: if bad news drops a share from RM20 to RM14 overnight, a stop at RM18 may execute far below RM18. A stop-limit order gives more control over the minimum price but may not execute at all if prices fall below its limit. The right order depends on whether the investor prioritises exiting the position or avoiding a poor price."

Mechanism + limitation + an alternative + a conditional conclusion.
