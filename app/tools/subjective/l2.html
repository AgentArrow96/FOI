The four-move answer (10–20 mark questions)

1. Establish the principle

Define the concept accurately in the chapter's terms: primary vs secondary, broker vs dealer, margin, short sale.

2. Explain the mechanism

Show why the relationship exists: the economic cause, not just the result.

3. Apply and analyse

Connect it to the scenario: who supplies the money, who owns the security, who owes what, and who bears the risk; show the numbers.

4. Evaluate and conclude

Weigh alternatives, name a limitation or assumption, and give a justified, conditional conclusion.

Technical Chapter 2 questions give marks for the formula, the substitution and the interpretation: compute margin or short-sale profit, then explain what it means for the investor.

Weak answer

Q: Evaluate whether margin trading is beneficial to investors.

"Margin trading allows investors to borrow money to buy stocks. It increases profits but is risky because investors may also lose money."

Describes margin but never explains the mechanism or reaches a conclusion.

Strong answer

"Margin trading lets an investor buy securities with a combination of personal equity and borrowed funds, the securities serving as collateral. Because the investor controls a larger position than the equity contributed, favourable price moves produce proportionally larger returns on equity. The same leverage magnifies losses, because the loan must still be repaid when prices fall; margin interest reduces net returns, and a decline may trigger a margin call. Margin trading may therefore suit investors who understand and can withstand leveraged losses, but it does not automatically improve investment performance."

Mechanism + consequences on both sides + a conditional conclusion.
