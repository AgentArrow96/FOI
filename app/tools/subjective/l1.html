The four-move answer (10–20 mark questions)

1. Establish the principle

Define the concept accurately in the chapter's terms: investment, return, risk, liquidity, suitability.

2. Explain the mechanism

Show why the relationship exists: the economic cause, not just the result.

3. Apply and analyse

Connect it to the scenario: the investor's goals, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk tolerance and capacity, taxes.

4. Evaluate and conclude

Weigh alternatives, name a limitation or assumption, and give a justified, conditional conclusion.

Most strong Chapter 1 answers explain a relationship: investor circumstances → objectives → constraints → asset choice → portfolio → monitoring.

Weak answer

Q: Explain why a young investor should not necessarily invest aggressively.

"Young investors can take more risks, but investing aggressively is dangerous because they could lose money."

Correct but thin: no framework, no exception, no recommendation.

Strong answer

"The lifecycle framework generally associates younger investors with growth-oriented securities because their longer horizons may give them greater capacity to recover from short-term market falls. However, age alone does not establish an investor's ability to bear risk. An individual with unstable income, inadequate emergency savings or near-term obligations may have limited capacity to withstand losses. The investor should therefore first meet the investment prerequisites and then choose investments consistent with the horizon, liquidity requirements and risk tolerance of each goal."

Principle + mechanism + exception + justified recommendation.
